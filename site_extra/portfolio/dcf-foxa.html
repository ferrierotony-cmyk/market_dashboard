Fox DCF model.

Built October 24, 2026. Excel, 323 formulas, 9 sources. Fiscal year ends June 30. Market data as of October 22, 2026.

A pure linear network company with no debt problem, so it isolates the one assumption that drives the Skydance bear case: how fast cable networks decline.

The question, the method, the finding, and what it means

The question
What is a linear network company worth on its own, with no debt to cloud the answer, and how fast does cable decline?
Data and method
Fiscal 2026 actuals of $17.1 billion of revenue and $3.9 billion of EBITDA, three revenue lines with an even year political cycle, margins drifting down as sports rights grow, and a 7.6 percent WACC.
What I found
The model gives $72.52 a share on the perpetuity method and $58.88 on the exit multiple, against a $68.27 price. The price implies 0.5 percent growth, and the market and I agree within a point.
What it means
Each point of annual cable decline is worth about $2 a share. That is the same lever as the Skydance bear case, without the $89 billion of debt, which is why Fox is the cleanest read of the five.

The clean read on linear decline

Skydance's bear case breaks because linear television shrinks 11 percent a year under $89 billion of debt. Fox is two thirds cable and broadcast networks with almost no net debt, so it shows what linear is worth on its own. Fiscal 2026 revenue still grew 5 percent with Super Bowl LX, so the decline is not a straight line.

Fox agreed in June 2026 to buy Roku for about $22 billion. The deal has not closed, so Roku is excluded.

The numbers

$72.52

The base case on the perpetuity method, at a 7.6 percent WACC and 1 percent long term growth. This is just above the $68.27 price.

$58.88

The base case on the exit multiple method, at 7 times FY2032 EBITDA. The market pays about 8 times today.

0.5%

The perpetual growth the price implies. The market and I agree on Fox within a point, which is the best check I have that the template works.

67%

The share of value that falls after FY2032, the lowest of the five. A long first year and flat cash flow put more of the value inside the forecast.

$42 to $97

The range from bear to bull across both methods. The bear case runs cable down 4 to 5 percent a year with margins at 17 percent, and the bull case holds margins at 23 and grows Tubi.

These are model outputs under stated assumptions. Nothing on this page is a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Inputs that differ from the template

  • Base year. Fiscal 2026 actuals: revenue $17.1 billion, adjusted EBITDA $3.9 billion, capex $502 million, depreciation $410 million.
  • Revenue. Three lines. Cable $7.5 billion, Television $9.9 billion and Other $400 million for FY2027. Growth alternates, up in even fiscal years with political advertising, Super Bowls and the World Cup, down in odd years.
  • EBITDA. Margin 22 percent drifting down to 20 as sports rights grow faster than revenue. Fiscal 2026 was 22.8 percent.
  • Cash flow. Capex 2.5 percent of revenue and a 0.5 percent working capital line for sports rights prepayments. Fiscal 2026 free cash flow fell to $1.5 billion from $3 billion with no explanation in the release, and the model does not assume it recovers all the way.
  • Discount rate. Unlevered beta 0.6, under 0.1 debt to equity, cost of equity 7.8 percent, cost of debt 6.2 percent. WACC 7.6 percent.
  • Equity bridge. $6.6 billion of debt, $4.2 billion of cash, 419.65 million shares from the 10-K cover, Class A and B together. The stub is 68 percent of FY2027.

Two pictures

I almost built the combined company

Roku adds $14.2 billion of cash consideration, a bridge loan, 143 million new shares and $400 million of synergies. Modelling that from a press release would mean guessing at the capital structure. I kept Fox standalone, and the Roku model will wait for the S-4.

In order of how much they move it

  • Cable decline. Each point of annual cable revenue decline is worth about $2 a share. This is the same lever as the Skydance bear case, without the debt.
  • Sports rights. The margin path assumes rights costs grow faster than revenue. If Fox lets a rights package go, margins would hold and the bull case becomes plausible.
  • Roku. Shareholders of both companies approved the deal in October 2026 and the Justice Department issued a second request, so closing is the first half of 2027 at the earliest. When it closes it changes the share count, the debt and the growth rate at once.
  • The missing FCF. The base case assumes about $2.4 billion of unlevered free cash flow a year. If fiscal 2026 was the new normal rather than timing, the base case is high by about $30 a share.
