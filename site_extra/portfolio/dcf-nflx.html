Netflix DCF model.

Built October 24, updated November 25, 2026. Excel, 312 formulas, 10 sources. Prices as of November 24.

Netflix is the streaming business the other four are trying to build. At 15 times EBITDA, what does the price already assume?

In short

The question
At 15 times EBITDA, what does the Netflix price assume?
Data and method
2026 guidance, content amortization kept in costs and a 10.3 percent WACC.
What I found
$52.02 and $69.97 a share, against $58.87. The price needs 4.0 percent growth forever or 11 times EBITDA in 2031.
What it means
The market prices it as the safest credit of the five and the richest equity.

    In pictures

    Why this company

    Skydance's base case grows streaming 9 percent fading to 4. Netflix grew 13 percent in 2026 on $51 billion of revenue at a 31.5 percent operating margin. If I assume Skydance can grow streaming, I should know what the leader earns doing it and what the market pays for it.

    Netflix walked away from Warner Bros. Discovery in February 2026 with a $2.9 billion termination fee. I exclude that fee from cash flow.

    How it is built
    • Base year. 2026 guidance of about $51.1 billion of revenue and a 31.5 percent operating margin from the October 20 shareholder letter, split into subscription and about $2.7 billion of advertising.
    • Revenue. Subscription grows 10 percent fading to 5. Advertising grows 40 percent fading to 15 off a small base, with 15 new ad markets in 2027.
    • EBITDA. Operating income plus property depreciation only. Content amortization, $16.4 billion in 2025, stays inside costs. The margin rises about a point a year to 36 percent by 2031.
    • Content cash. Netflix spends about 1.1 times what it amortizes. The excess, about 3.3 percent of revenue, sits in the working capital and content line and fades to 2 percent as spending growth slows.
    • Discount rate. Unlevered beta 1.2, almost no leverage, so the WACC is close to the 10.4 percent cost of equity. Cost of debt 5.8 percent, the July 2026 notes repriced about 85 bp over today's Treasury.
    • Equity bridge. $15.3 billion of gross debt, $8.2 billion of cash and 4,106 million shares, from the 10-Q cover. Stock compensation is already in operating income, so it is not taken out again.
    What I got wrong first

    A beta of 1.61 taken from a quote page gave an 11.6 percent WACC and $42 a share. That beta is a five year regression on a stock that fell by half in 2022. I used 1.2 and recorded the reasoning, and the sensitivity grid runs from 8 to 11 percent so that a reader can restore the 1.61.

    What would change the answer
    • The exit multiple. Each turn of EBITDA is worth about $4 a share. 14 times is below the 15 the market pays today and above where a company growing 5 percent should trade in 2031.
    • Margin ceiling. The base case stops at 36 percent. Every point of terminal margin is worth about $1.50 a share.
    • Advertising. The October 20 letter cut 2026 ad guidance from about $3 billion to $2.7 billion. Advertising is 5 percent of revenue. It is a small line that the market treats like a big one.
    • The share count. On the diluted 4,195 million, every value on this page is about 2 percent lower.
    Since the model
    • The price. $58.87 on November 24, from $65.85 in October. It moved toward the model.
    • Two corrections. October took stock compensation out twice, worth about $1 a share, and used 58 million shares too many.
    • Buybacks. $4.8 billion in the third quarter, well above a quarter of the year's free cash flow guidance.
