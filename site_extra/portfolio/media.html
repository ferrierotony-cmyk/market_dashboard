Project

Media DCF models and credit comps.

Built October 24 and 25, 2026. Five Excel models plus a credit comps workbook. 1,860 formulas, 71 sources. Market data as of October 22 and 23, 2026.

Five companies, one number first.

Five media companies valued the same way, then lined up the way a desk lines them up: leverage, coverage, cash flow against debt, the equity cushion under the bonds, and where the paper prices.

What I am interested in and what I do

The primary market, and what makes a bond price where it prices

I want a debt capital markets or syndicate seat, or fixed income research. What I care about is the moment a new bond gets priced: where the talk starts, how the book builds, where it prints, what it does the next day. It is the part of finance where the numbers get checked against the market within hours.

At Finsight I spent nine months as close to that as an intern gets: order books from 30 or more syndicate desks on 15 to 20 deals a day, price talk to print, volume against Street estimates, and headlines on more than 25 liability management deals.

This page is five companies I covered or watched, valued the same way and then read as credits. Skydance is the anchor because I covered the Paramount tender and then watched the market reprice the whole capital structure when the Warner Bros. Discovery debt came.

A desk starts with net debt to EBITDA, then coverage, then years of free cash flow to repay the debt, then how much equity sits under the bonds if EBITDA misses. Skydance fails every test the other four pass. Net debt is 68 percent of enterprise value, free cash flow after interest is about zero in year one, and a 31 percent EBITDA miss leaves nothing for the equity.

That is why the first lien 2031s trade at 272 bp, 170 bp wide of the BBB index, and the second liens at 483 bp, while Netflix printed a 10 year at 5.346 percent in July. The spreads in the table are where I think a new 10 year prints for each name. Only Skydance's is anchored on sourced levels; the other four are my inputs, labelled in the workbook.

The deleveraging question

Can Skydance get to 3.0 times on cash flow alone

Management's target is 3.0 times by 2029. On my base case, with every free dollar going to debt, net leverage is 6.3 times at the end of 2027, 5.0 at the end of 2029 and 4.5 at the end of 2031. Getting to 3.0 means cutting about $47 billion of net debt on under a billion of first year free cash flow after interest. That is asset sales or equity, which is what the second lien equity clawback is for.

What is the same and what is not

The rules I held constant

  • Same discount rate inputs. 10 year Treasury 4.95 percent, equity risk premium 4.5 percent, cash tax 23 percent, market weights, Hamada relevering. Cost of debt 75 to 125 bp over Treasuries for the four investment grade names, 8 percent blended for Skydance.
  • Same cash flow definition. Adjusted EBITDA less stock compensation, cash restructuring, cash taxes on profit after tax depreciation, capex and a working capital and content line. Stock compensation is a real cost in all five.
  • Same mechanics. Mid year discounting, a first year stub, perpetuity and exit multiple side by side, three scenarios on one switch.
  • Different fiscal years. Disney and Fox run FY2027 to FY2032 with stubs of 0.94 and 0.68. The other three run calendar 2026 to 2031 with a 0.19 stub.
  • Different pending deals. Comcast before the NBCUniversal and Sky spin. Fox without Roku. Skydance after Warner Bros. Discovery, because that one closed.
  • Interest expense. Comcast's and Disney's are reported. Skydance's, Netflix's and Fox's are estimates from gross debt and a blended coupon, flagged in the comps.

What I got wrong first

Betas off a quote page, and a cost of debt below the Treasury plus nothing

Quoted betas as unlevered betas. Netflix came out at an 11.6 percent WACC and $42 a share, and Comcast's unlevered below a utility's. I set them by hand, 0.6 for Fox, 0.7 for Comcast, 0.9 for Skydance, 1.05 for Disney, 1.2 for Netflix, and wrote down why on each WACC sheet.

Then the one a desk would catch first: the four investment grade names borrowing at 5 to 5.5 percent with the 10 year at 4.95, which is 5 to 55 bp of spread. Nobody prints there. I moved them to 75 to 125 bp over. It barely touches three of them and takes $3 off Comcast, because half its capital is debt.

On October 26 I checked those inputs against where each name's long paper actually trades, on a terminal. Disney, Netflix and Fox hold within the noise. Comcast is the miss: after three agencies put it on negative watch for the spin, the market has it wider than my 95 bp by a margin that would not surprise a desk. Moving Comcast's cost of debt to where the market is lifts its WACC to 7.8 percent and takes about $2 more off the perpetuity value. It changes no conclusion, because the model still lands far above the price. The levels themselves stay off this site.
