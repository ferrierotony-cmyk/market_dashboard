Project

Media DCF models and credit comps.

Built October 24 and 25, 2026. Five Excel models plus a credit comps workbook. 1,860 formulas, 71 sources. Market data as of October 22 and 23, 2026.

Five media companies valued the same way, then lined up the way a desk lines them up: leverage, coverage, cash flow against debt, the equity cushion under the bonds, and where the paper prices.

What I am interested in and what I do

The primary market, and what makes a bond price where it prices

I want to work on a debt capital markets or syndicate desk, or in fixed income research. What I care about is the moment a new bond gets priced: where the talk starts, how the book builds, where it prints, and what it does the next day. That is the part of finance where the numbers are checked against the market within hours.

At Finsight I spent nine months as close to that as an intern can get. I collected order books and allocations from 30 or more syndicate desks on 15 to 20 deals a day, tracked initial price talk to the print, kept the daily tally of volume against Street estimates with book attrition and new issue concession, and wrote headlines on more than 25 liability management deals. High yield in the spring, investment grade over the summer.

On my own time I build the pieces of that job that can run on public data. A dashboard that scores whether the new issue window is open. A tracker that reads tender and exchange offers out of SEC filings every day. Research on whether green bonds price tighter at issue. And this page: five companies I covered or watched, valued and then read as credits. Skydance is the anchor because I covered the Paramount tender at Finsight and then watched the market reprice the whole capital structure when the Warner Bros. Discovery debt came.

A desk does not start with the equity. It starts with net debt to EBITDA, then asks how many times EBITDA covers interest, how many years of free cash flow it takes to repay the debt, and how much equity sits under the bonds if EBITDA misses. Skydance fails every one of those tests that the other four pass. Its $84 billion of net debt is 68 percent of its enterprise value. Its free cash flow after interest in the first year is about zero. A 31 percent EBITDA miss at today's multiple leaves nothing for the equity.

That is why the first lien 2031s trade at 272 bp, 170 bp wide of the BBB index, and the second liens at 483 bp, while Netflix printed a 10 year at 5.346 percent in July. The spreads in the table are where I think a new 10 year would price for each name. Only Skydance's is anchored on sourced trading levels. The other four are my inputs at 75 to 125 bp over the Treasury, and the comps workbook says so next to each one.

The deleveraging question

Can Skydance get to 3.0 times on cash flow alone

Management's target is 3.0 times net leverage by 2029. On my base case, with synergies realized and every dollar of free cash flow after interest and the dividend going to debt, net leverage is 6.3 times at the end of 2027, 5.0 at the end of 2029 and 4.5 at the end of 2031. To reach 3.0 the company needs to cut about $47 billion of net debt, and its first year free cash flow after interest is under a billion. That means asset sales or equity, which is what the second lien equity clawback is for and what every syndicate desk asked when the $52 billion priced.

What is the same and what is not

The rules I held constant

  • Same discount rate inputs. 10 year Treasury at 4.95 percent on October 22, 2026, a 4.5 percent equity risk premium, a 23 percent cash tax rate, market value weights and Hamada relevering of an unlevered beta. Cost of debt set 75 to 125 bp over the Treasury for the four investment grade names and 8 percent blended for Skydance.
  • Same cash flow definition. Adjusted EBITDA less stock compensation, cash restructuring, cash taxes on profit after tax depreciation, capex and a working capital and content line. Stock compensation is a real cost in all five.
  • Same mechanics. Mid year discounting, a stub for the first partial year, perpetuity and exit multiple methods side by side, and three scenarios on one switch.
  • Different fiscal years. Disney ends in late September and Fox in June, so their forecasts run FY2027 to FY2032 and the stub fractions are 0.94 and 0.68. Netflix, Comcast and Skydance run calendar 2026 to 2031 with a 0.19 stub.
  • Different pending deals. Comcast is valued before the NBCUniversal and Sky spin announced for mid 2027. Fox is valued without Roku, which closes in the first half of 2027. Skydance is valued after Warner Bros. Discovery, because that one closed.
  • Interest expense. Comcast's and Disney's are reported. Skydance's, Netflix's and Fox's are estimates from gross debt and a blended coupon, flagged in the comps.

The company pages

One page per model

  • Skydance. The anchor. Pro forma for Warner Bros. Discovery, $89 billion of gross debt, a synergy line that is all of the EBITDA growth, and a bear case where the debt is worth more than the business.
  • Netflix. A 10.3 percent WACC and 17 times EBITDA. Net cash in practice. The perpetuity method cannot get to the price. The exit method can.
  • Disney. Three segments, a fiscal year that ends in September, cruise ships that explain the capex, and a single A balance sheet.
  • Comcast. The one the model cannot reconcile. 2.3 times leverage, a 7.5 percent WACC, half its capital in debt, and a price that implies a shrinking business.
  • Fox. Almost no net debt, 23 percent margins, even year political advertising, and a $13 billion Roku financing left out on purpose.

What I got wrong first

Betas off a quote page, and a cost of debt below the Treasury plus nothing

My first pass took each company's quoted beta as the unlevered beta. Netflix came out at an 11.6 percent WACC and $42 a share. Comcast's 0.67 unlevered to 0.4 once I backed out its leverage, which would have given it a lower cost of equity than a utility. So I set the unlevered betas by hand, 0.6 for Fox, 0.7 for Comcast, 0.9 for Skydance, 1.05 for Disney and 1.2 for Netflix, and wrote down why on each WACC sheet.

The second mistake was the one a desk would catch first. I had the four investment grade names borrowing at 5 to 5.5 percent with the 10 year at 4.95, which is 5 to 55 bp of spread. Nobody prints there. I moved them to 75 to 125 bp over. It barely touches Netflix, Disney and Fox. It takes Comcast's WACC from 7.2 to 7.5 percent and $3 off its perpetuity value, because half its capital is debt.
