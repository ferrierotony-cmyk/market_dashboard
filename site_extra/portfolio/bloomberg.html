One pull in detail

A professor asked whether the terminal had ESG scores. I screened every US listed company with a Bloomberg ESG score, pulled ten years of history plus the current MSCI, Sustainalytics and S&P scores, and sent a values only file with a field guide tab. I made two mistakes before getting it right. The first list was built by hand and contained duplicate tickers, and the first file still had live Bloomberg formulas in it.

How I run the team
  • Lanes. I own the book, the final decision on every trade and the sizing. Two analysts pitch names with a one line summary covering what the company is, why now, the catalyst and its date, and what would invalidate the idea. One member keeps the trade log and another owns the earnings calendar.
  • Entry rule. We only buy a stock that is above its open and beating the WLS benchmark that morning. We do not buy names that are falling.
  • Sizing and exits. We keep position caps below the 20 percent limit and write down an exit for every name before it enters the book. The log records the reason for every trade.
  • The screens. EQS for candidates, EE and ANR for what is priced in, the earnings calendar, N and CN for news, GP for charts, PORT for exposures. My R dashboard points at the book for the daily snapshot.
What past winners did

Past winners focused on earnings and catalysts inside the window. They did not sit in safe long term holdings. RIT's 2024 team leaned into volatility and foreign names. USF's 2025 captain ran an earnings volatility book. The fall 2025 grand prize team from the Chinese University of Hong Kong won on discipline and Bloomberg's own data.

The cautionary example is a team that took a large early bet, rose 18 percent, moved to cash and was not invited to the finals because the judges decided it was not investing. For that reason every name in our book has a written thesis, and we keep the log clean enough to show a judge.

What I am learning

My background is in bonds. A long only equity book scored against an index is a different game. Two weeks of it taught me more about sizing, and about passing on a trade, than anything I had read. The rule that kept us out of tech during CPI week is now my risk management example.
