One pull in detail

ESG scores for 2,172 US listed companies

A professor asked whether the terminal had ESG scores. It did. I built a screen of every US exchange listed company with a Bloomberg ESG score, pulled ten years of history plus the current MSCI, Sustainalytics and S&P scores, and sent it as a values only file with a field guide tab that explains each column and what it cannot be used for. I got it wrong twice first: duplicate tickers in a hand built list, and a file that still had live Bloomberg formulas in it, which breaks on any computer without a terminal.

The Trading Challenge

The only university competition run entirely inside the terminal

$1M

Virtual starting capital per team. Long only, single name stocks from the WLS index, no ETFs, no leverage.

WLS

The benchmark: Bloomberg World Large, Mid and Small Cap Price Return Index. Teams are ranked on relative P&L against it, not raw return, so in a down market not losing can win.

20%

Most any one position can be of the book. We cap ours lower so one bad name cannot sink the window.

2,600+

Teams in the fall 2025 edition, more than 11,000 students from 50 countries, 72,000 virtual trades. The grand prize team returned more than 400 percent.

8 weeks

Roughly the trading cycle. Winners are announced around the end of November, so most of the return has to come from things that happen inside the window.

Oct 12

The day our book opened for the fall 2026 round.

The Bloomberg Global Trading Challenge gives each team a million virtual dollars and about eight weeks. You can only buy single name stocks in the WLS index, long only, no ETFs, no leverage, nothing above 20 percent of the book. The winner is the team with the highest relative P&L against the WLS index, which changes everything about how you play it. Beating a falling index by holding up is worth as much as beating a rising one by running ahead of it.

UMass Lowell's teams are run by Professor Weicker. I was an analyst on the spring 2026 round, which ran February to April, and I captain the fall 2026 team. Our book opened on October 12.

How I run the team

Four lanes and a rule set written down before the first trade

  • Lanes. I own the portfolio view, final call on every trade and the sizing, and I watch the leaderboard. Two analysts pitch names with a written one liner: what it is, why now, the catalyst and date, what kills it. One person keeps the trade log and the dashboard. One person owns the earnings and events calendar.
  • Entry rule. We only buy a stock if it is above its open and beating the WLS benchmark that morning. It keeps us from catching falling names and from buying into a tape that is already against us.
  • Sizing and exits. Position caps below the 20 percent limit, a plan for how fast cash goes to work, and a written exit on every name before it goes in. The trade log carries the reason for every entry and exit so we can defend each one later.
  • The screens. EQS to build the candidate list, EE and ANR to see what is already priced in, the earnings calendar on every holding and candidate, N and CN for news, GP for charts and PORT for live exposures. My R dashboard points at the watchlist and the book so we get a daily snapshot without pulling it by hand.

The book so far

Two energy names and four passes

On October 12 we bought Exxon and Chevron after oil bounced. Both cleared the entry rule. We passed on Goldman Sachs, Nvidia, ASML and General Dynamics the same week. General Dynamics missed the rule by 39 cents on October 15, so it stayed off. We also stayed out of tech after the hot CPI and PPI prints and the chip selloff, which is the pass I would defend hardest. The exit on Chevron is written down: close it at $225, or earlier if Brent falls below $102.

I am not going to post a running return here. The competition is scored on the WLS index at the end of the window and the leaderboard moves every day. When it is over, the result goes here, good or bad.

What past winners did

Volatility with a thesis, not volatility alone

I read what I could find on past winners before the window opened. The through line is earnings and catalysts inside the window, not safe long term picks. RIT's 2024 team beat more than 2,400 teams by leaning into volatility and foreign names. USF's 2025 captain ran an earnings volatility book across tech, healthcare, energy and rare earths. The fall 2025 grand prize team from the Chinese University of Hong Kong won on discipline and heavy use of Bloomberg's own data, not on wild swings.

The cautionary tale is the team that gambled early, got up 18 percent, parked in cash and was not invited to the finals because the judges decided it was not investing. That is why every name in our book has a written thesis before it goes in. The entry rule is there to make the volatility work for us. The log is there so it survives a judge.

What I am learning

Equities are not my home market, and that is the point

My work is bonds. Order books, spreads, new issue pricing. A long only equity book scored against an index is a different game, and the first two weeks taught me more about position sizing and about passing on a trade than anything I read. The rule that kept us out of tech on the CPI week is the example I now use when someone asks me about risk management.
