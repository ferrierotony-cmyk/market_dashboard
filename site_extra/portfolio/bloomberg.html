Bloomberg at UMass Lowell.

Terminal Assistant, September 2026 to now. Trading Challenge captain, fall 2026. Investment Analyst, spring 2026 round.

I train business school students on the Bloomberg Terminal, pull data for faculty research, and captain the UML team in the Bloomberg Global Trading Challenge. Both roles use the same room and the same terminals, but they are two different jobs.

Training and data pulls

  • I train business school students across 10 classes on functions from SRCH and YAS to curves and spreads, including bond screens, yield and spread checks and company financials.
  • I pull Bloomberg data for faculty research and class assignments. The green bond research on this site began as one of those requests.

ESG scores for 2,172 US listed companies

A professor asked whether the terminal had ESG scores. I screened every US listed company with a Bloomberg ESG score, pulled ten years of history plus the current MSCI, Sustainalytics and S&P scores, and sent a values only file with a field guide tab. I made two mistakes before getting it right. The first list was built by hand and contained duplicate tickers, and the first file still had live Bloomberg formulas in it.

A university competition run entirely inside the terminal

$1M

Virtual starting capital for each team. The book is long only and limited to single name stocks from the WLS index, with no ETFs and no leverage.

WLS

The benchmark is the Bloomberg World Large, Mid and Small Cap Price Return Index. Teams are ranked on relative P&L against it and not on raw return, so in a falling market a team can win by losing less than the index.

20%

The largest share of the book that any one position may hold. We set our own cap lower so that a single poor position cannot determine the result.

2,600+

Teams in the fall 2025 edition, with more than 11,000 students from 50 countries and 72,000 virtual trades. The grand prize team returned more than 400 percent.

8 weeks

The approximate length of the trading cycle. Winners are announced around the end of November, so most of the return has to come from events that occur inside the window.

Oct 12

The day our book opened for the fall 2026 round.

Scoring on relative P&L against the WLS index changes the approach. Beating a falling index by holding up is worth as much as beating a rising one by running ahead of it.

UMass Lowell's teams are run by Professor Weicker. I was an analyst on the spring 2026 round and I captain the fall 2026 team.

Four lanes and a rule set written down before the first trade

  • Lanes. I own the book, the final decision on every trade and the sizing. Two analysts pitch names with a one line summary covering what the company is, why now, the catalyst and its date, and what would invalidate the idea. One member keeps the trade log and another owns the earnings calendar.
  • Entry rule. We only buy a stock that is above its open and beating the WLS benchmark that morning. We do not buy names that are falling.
  • Sizing and exits. We keep position caps below the 20 percent limit and write down an exit for every name before it enters the book. The log records the reason for every trade.
  • The screens. EQS for candidates, EE and ANR for what is priced in, the earnings calendar, N and CN for news, GP for charts, PORT for exposures. My R dashboard points at the book for the daily snapshot.

Two energy names and four passes

On October 12 we bought Exxon and Chevron after oil rebounded. We passed on Goldman Sachs, Nvidia, ASML and General Dynamics the same week, and General Dynamics missed the entry rule by 39 cents on October 15. We stayed out of technology after the high CPI and PPI prints and the selloff in chip stocks, which is the decision I would defend most strongly. Chevron's exit is written down at $225, or earlier if Brent falls below $102.

I do not post a running return here. The result will be published when the window closes, whatever it is.

Volatility backed by a thesis

What past winners have in common is a focus on earnings and catalysts inside the window, as opposed to safe long term holdings. RIT's 2024 team leaned into volatility and foreign names. USF's 2025 captain ran an earnings volatility book. The fall 2025 grand prize team from the Chinese University of Hong Kong won on discipline and Bloomberg's own data.

The cautionary example is a team that took a large early bet, rose 18 percent, moved to cash and was not invited to the finals because the judges decided it was not investing. For that reason every name in our book has a written thesis, and the log is kept to a standard that would hold up to a judge's review.

Equities are not my home market

My background is in bonds. A long only equity book scored against an index is a different discipline, and two weeks of it taught me more about sizing and about passing on a trade than anything I had read. The rule that kept us out of technology during CPI week is now the example I give when asked about risk management.
