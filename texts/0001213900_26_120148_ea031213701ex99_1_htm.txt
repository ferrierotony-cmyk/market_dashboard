We consider it important to clarify that the circumstances referred to in Requests 2.2.1 through 2.2.4, when analyzed in accordance with the explanations provided in the preceding responses, do not constitute conflicts of interest. Consistent with the statements made in the Board of Directors’ Report and reiterated in the Amended Report, Mr. Nadal has no interest contrary to that of the Company in connection with the Offers, given that: (i) he does not currently hold, nor did he hold as of the issuance date of the Board of Directors’ Report, any position or powers at Aspi, nor does he participate in the provision of services under the Management Agreement; (ii) his status as a shareholder places him in the same position as the other recipients of the Offers, with interests aligned with those of the minority shareholders; and (iii) the incentive referred to in Request 2.2.3 arises from his employment relationship with the Company, is not conditional upon the Offers or their outcome, and does not give rise to any economic interest on his part in their success or failure. Accordingly, Mr. Nadal’s decision to abstain was not motivated by the aforementioned circumstances, but solely by prudential considerations. Although his position as a principal executive officer of the Company does not, in itself, imply an interest contrary to that of the Company in this particular case, it does establish a relationship between Mr. Nadal and the Company. Therefore, it constitutes a recommended corporate governance practice for directors who are also members of senior management to abstain from participating in the evaluation of a tender offer for the Company’s own shares. This practice seeks to ensure that, in the context of a tender offer, the board of directors’ report primarily reflects the perspective of directors who perform supervisory functions over the Company’s management, thereby reinforcing the separation of responsibilities between the Board of Directors, as the supervisory body, and management, as the body responsible for the ordinary course of business, without calling into question the objectivity of directors who are also members of management. 5 Furthermore, the abstention of directors who are members of management prevents any perception among the recipients of the Offers and the market generally that the evaluation of the Peruvian Tender Offer could be influenced by considerations related to the Company’s current management, even where no such considerations exist, thereby strengthening shareholders’ confidence in the objectivity of the report. For this reason, Mr. Nadal deemed it prudent to abstain so that the evaluation of the Peruvian Tender Offer would exclusively reflect the judgment of directors who are not involved in the Company’s management and ordinary business operations. Accordingly, this constitutes a preventive measure intended to reinforce the independence and transparency of the process, rather than an acknowledgment of a conflict of interest within the meaning of Article 180 of the LGS. Furthermore, in response to the Company’s request for conflict-of-interest disclosures, Mr. Nadal did not report any additional circumstances, and the Company is not aware of any circumstances that could constitute a conflict of interest in connection with the Offers. Notwithstanding the foregoing, and in response to the requirements of the Office, clarifications expressly setting forth the reasons why Mr. Nadal does not have a conflict of interest have been incorporated into Section 5 of the Amended Report. 2.3. Reevaluate the participation of Mr. Raimundo Morales Dasso in the preparation, deliberation and approval of the Board of Directors’ Report, considering that he holds shares and ADSs of Pacasmayo and simultaneously serves as Vice Chairman of the Board of Directors of the target company; and that, pursuant to the information disclosed in “SCHEDULE 14D-9 (Amendment No. 1)” filed with the SEC, he is one of the shareholders who has expressed his intention to accept the offer, which is inconsistent with the duty of neutrality he is required to observe under Article 56(c) of the Tender Offer Regulations. Consequently, in accordance with the LGS, he should abstain from participating in the deliberation and approval of the Board of Directors’ Report. In response to the Office’s request, the Board of Directors has reevaluated Mr. Morales’ participation in the deliberation and approval of the Board of Directors’ Report and has reaffirmed that Mr. Morales has no conflict of interest in connection with the Offers and that there has been no breach of the duty of neutrality. As stated in Section 5 of the Board of Directors’ Report, Mr. Morales holds shares and ADSs of the Company representing less than one percent (1%) of its share capital. It should be noted that Article 15 of the Tender Offer Regulations does not require the board of directors’ report to disclose holdings of securities of the target company by its directors. Mr. Morales’ holdings were disclosed solely in accordance with the highest standards of transparency, as expressly stated in the Board of Directors’ Report, particularly considering that Mr. Morales would participate in the vote on the relevant resolution. This demonstrates that, under the applicable regulations, a director’s status as a shareholder of the target company does not, in itself, constitute a circumstance that is relevant to the recipients of the offer or a situation giving rise to a conflict of interest. Such ownership places Mr. Morales in the same position as the other recipients of the Offers, with interests aligned with those of the minority shareholders and not contrary to those of the Company. Accordingly, the circumstances contemplated under Article 180 of the LGS, which requires a director to abstain when he or she has an interest contrary to that of the company, do not arise. On the contrary, his status as a minority shareholder reinforces the alignment of his interests with those of the recipients of the Offers (namely, obtaining the best possible price), whose protection is precisely the purpose of the report contemplated under Article 15 of the Tender Offer Regulations. Similarly, Mr. Morales’ decision to accept the Offers with respect to his own securities constitutes a personal investment decision made in his capacity as a shareholder rather than as a member of the Board of Directors. Such decision does not constitute an act of the Company or its governing bodies, nor is it capable of disrupting the conduct of the Offers or favoring the Offeror. Therefore, it does not constitute a breach of the duty of neutrality established under Article 56(c) of the Tender Offer Regulations. Furthermore, the content of the Board of Directors’ Report reflects such neutrality: it does not contain any recommendation regarding acceptance of the Peruvian Tender Offer, presents its advantages and disadvantages in a balanced manner, and the Board of Directors adopted a neutral position regarding the U.S. Tender Offer, leaving the decision to participate in the Offers entirely to the discretion of each securities holder. It should also be noted that the Board of Directors’ Report was prepared and preliminarily approved by the special committee of independent directors, of which Mr. Morales is not a member. 6 With respect to the duty of neutrality of CPAC’s Board of Directors, it should be noted that the Tender Offer Regulations establish that the Board of Directors must act with absolute neutrality toward potential competing offers, “prioritizing the interests of shareholders at all times and refraining from carrying out or agreeing to any act outside the ordinary course of the Company’s business that would have the effect of disrupting the normal conduct of the offer or favoring any offeror, such as the issuance of shares or other securities, the execution of option agreements or the disposal of corporate assets, among others.” In this regard, a director’s personal decision to accept a tender offer cannot be considered detrimental to the interests of the issuer’s shareholders (particularly in the present case, where the Peruvian Tender Offer has been launched for 100% of the Company’s shares), nor can it be considered an act outside the ordinary course of the Company’s business, or an act that would disrupt the normal conduct of the offer or favor any offeror. Moreover, the disclosure of Mr. Morales’ intention to accept the Offers was made in compliance with the U.S. regulations applicable to the U.S. Tender Offer, which require Schedule 14D-9 to disclose whether, after reasonable inquiry and to the target company’s knowledge, its directors and principal executive officers intend to accept the offer with respect to securities held by them. Such information was included in Section 8 of the Board of Directors’ Report so that its content could be incorporated into Schedule 14D-9, and responds to an international regulatory requirement, without implying any acknowledgment of a conflict of interest. Consequently, following the reevaluation conducted, and considering that there is neither a conflict of interest nor any breach of the duty of neutrality, Mr. Morales has participated in the deliberation and approval of the Amended Report, just as he participated in the deliberation and approval of the Board of Directors’ Report. Neither the Company, nor the Board of Directors, nor Mr. Morales considers that any conflict of interest exists that would justify his abstention under Article 180 of the LGS. 2.4. Regarding conflicts of interest and the participation of directors in the preparation and approval of the report Article 15 of the Tender Offer Regulations requires the Board of Directors or the highest governing body of the target company to issue a reasoned report setting forth the advantages and disadvantages of accepting the Tender Offer, detailing its observations in favor of or against the offer, as well as information provided by members of the Board of Directors, the Chief Executive Officer, or the principal executives of the target company that could indicate the existence of any actual or potential conflicts of interest they may have in connection with the offer, among other information. Furthermore, Article 171 of Law No. 26887, the General Corporations Law (hereinafter, the “GCL”), establishes that directors must perform their duties with the diligence of a prudent businessperson and a loyal representative. In turn, Article 173 of the aforementioned law provides that directors elected by a group or class of shareholders owe the same duties to the company and the other shareholders as all other directors, and that their actions may not be limited to defending the interests of those who elected them. Along the same lines, Article 180 of the GCL provides that any director who has an interest in any matter that conflicts with the interests of the company must disclose such interest and abstain from participating in the deliberation and decision-making concerning that matter. In light of the foregoing, the duty of loyalty constitutes a fiduciary obligation whereby directors of a company must prioritize the interests of the company over their own interests or those of third parties or business groups, and must abstain from participating when such interests conflict with those of the company. Additionally, Article 56(c) of the Tender Offer Regulations establishes that, from the moment the issuer’s governing bodies become aware of the potential launch of a Tender Offer until the publication of its results, they must act with absolute neutrality, prioritizing the interests of shareholders at all times and refraining from carrying out or arranging any actions outside the ordinary course of business of the company that could disrupt the normal conduct of the offer or favor any bidder. In light of the foregoing, it should be noted that the requirement for the Board of Directors or the highest governing body of the company to issue a report addressing the advantages and disadvantages of the offer—including observations in favor of or against the offer—is not intended to serve a merely descriptive purpose, but rather to ensure that the recipients of the offer have the information necessary to assess whether or not accepting it would be advisable. For this reason, in the context of a tender offer, compliance with the duties of loyalty, diligence, and neutrality by members of the Board of Directors when issuing their opinion is of utmost importance. In this regard, although the obligation to disclose the existence of conflicts of interest established under the applicable regulations constitutes a measure consistent with the principle of transparency of information to the recipients of the offer, the mere disclosure of such conflicts of interest does not neutralize or eliminate their effects, nor does it, in itself, establish that such conflicts are irrelevant for purposes of assessing a director’s participation in the preparation and approval of the report. Abstention is the only mechanism intended to prevent a particular interest from influencing the decision-making process of the corporate governing body. Av.
