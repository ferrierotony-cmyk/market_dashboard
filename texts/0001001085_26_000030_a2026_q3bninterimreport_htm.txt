Evolution is Essential One of the most fundamental requirements for long-term success is the ability to evolve in response to a changing environment. In nature, survival and progress depend on adaptation. Through the process of evolution, organisms become better suited to their environment, with their strengths becoming more prevalent over successive generations. As environmental conditions change, so too must the characteristics that enable organisms to survive and thrive. Evolution, therefore, is not simply a process of improvement, but a continuous process of adaptation to remain relevant and viable. The same principle applies to business. Markets evolve, customer expectations shift, technologies advance, and competitive landscapes are constantly reshaped. What drives success today may not be what sustains it tomorrow. Businesses that remain anchored to the conditions, strategies, and capabilities that once made them successful risk losing their relevance as the world around them changes. Long-term success, therefore, depends not only on the ability to perform well in the present, but on the willingness and capacity to continually adapt, transform, and evolve for the future. The ability to evolve is one of the defining characteristics of businesses that endure and thrive over generations. It is the difference between becoming Berkshire Hathaway, or becoming Kodak Cameras. Recall that Amazon started as a bookseller, Alphabet was once only Google, Meta began as Facebook, and NVIDIA used its chips largely for gaming applications. As for Brookfield, over the past 40+ years, we have adhered to the same very basic principles of investing — which are to invest to earn cash flows, with good people and partners, in good countries, and in world-class assets. Despite a consistent approach to our business over this period, we have continually evolved our strategy as the world has changed. This is exemplified by the fact that approximately 50% of our total $1.3 trillion of assets under management are in asset classes that did not exist at investable scale 15 years ago. Our business has evolved across the board — in our corporate form, capital structure, and investment sectors. Our funding model has evolved through the building of our alternative asset management business in the 2000s, the launch of our public affiliates in the 2010s, our insurance business in 2020, and our current focus on expanding access to private markets for individual investors. Our corporate structure has also changed as we created new businesses and adapted to changing capital markets. Our investment focus has also evolved as infrastructure investment has expanded into new areas, including ai and the buildout of its supporting infrastructure, but the same is true of energy, alternative real estate, batteries, and telecom towers. Over 20 years, we have also moved ever so slowly into the infrastructure supporting technology but given that the physical world is now colliding head-on with technology, we are now at the heart of building the physical backbone that enables it, providing energy and compute infrastructure to the best companies globally and using that knowledge to invest around these themes. This also has the added benefit of allowing us to be at the forefront of using technology to make our businesses better. It is the ability to continually adapt and evolve (not wholesale transformation) that makes the difference between good and great (or not so great) companies. Wholesale transformations work once in a while, but more often large mistakes are made during the process due to incorrect assumptions or flawed execution. On the other hand, a series of smaller, deliberate changes allow a company to adapt and learn over time. As a result, if successfully executed, this approach has a far greater chance of success. 6 BROOKFIELD CORPORATION Organizations cannot stop the world from changing. The best one can do is continually evolve. Great companies change before they need to. Lucky companies manage to adjust when the world turns against them and survive. The rest are left behind and eventually become just a name in history. The Best is Yet to Come for Westinghouse The electricity markets of most countries today are strained due to electrification of industry, lack of new-build generation capacity, reliance on foreign inputs, and years of neglect of grids. Solar, wind, gas, and other power sources are each important but cannot provide the same benefits as nuclear power. There is no other source of power that can be built which is baseload (runs all hours), can be built at scale (a two-unit nuclear plant produces ~2,300 MW), is carbon-free, can store fuel for up to five years on-site (enabling energy security and reliability unlike anything else) and can last up to 80 years (as close to perpetual as energy gets). Nuclear plants currently form the critical backbone of energy infrastructure in many countries and are very critical to their reliability and resilience. But to meet the demands of electrification, energy security, and the buildout of ai infrastructure, more global nuclear capacity is needed. Westinghouse technology is used by roughly half of the world's operating nuclear reactors. The company also services over half of the global reactor fleet, with the majority of its cash flows today generated from recurring fuel supply and maintenance services. Our new Westinghouse AP1000 technology builds on decades of safety records and, with the scaling up of manufacturing, can now be built at a scale, which should bring down both costs and construction timelines. Westinghouse is the design and engineering behind many of the most advanced and proven large-scale newly constructed reactors globally and it does not appear that anyone else will ever surpass them. Westinghouse has built six new reactors in the last few years, and the technology is operating at historically high levels of efficiency and safety. Rarely have we owned a business that governments want to ensure are successful because it is critical to their country’s success. In particular, the U.S. government has been extremely helpful to Westinghouse in order to bring nuclear power online faster and at lower cost. Building on our strategic partnership with the U.S. Department of Commerce announced last year, the U.S. Department of Energy announced a $17.5 billion commitment to finance the procurement of long-lead-time equipment for a further ten new large-scale reactors. By supporting early equipment procurement, this should materially lower execution risk, shorten development timelines, and establish a repeatable model for large-scale nuclear construction in the U.S. and around the world. It is already making a difference. More recently, Westinghouse agreed to a multi-faceted deal with the Korean and U.S. governments. Korea will invest up to $120 billion into reactors constructed on U.S. federal lands. This is substantial progress on our deal announced last year and further brings the needed historic levels of new-build nuclear construction closer to reality. Second, Korea has agreed to bring its supply chain to the U.S. to scale up production of plants. We need more talented construction groups, and Korea has some excellent ones. Third, we will allow Korea to build one set of its own reactors, on a one-time exemption basis, in the U.S., and Westinghouse will earn enhanced licensing fees for this build as their technology uses Westinghouse's intellectual property, which Korean companies are currently not permitted to use in the U.S. In total, Westinghouse has 14 reactors in various phases of construction, another 40 coming, and 80+ behind that. This is one of the largest buildouts of energy ever. This also does not include various other countries that are also restarting discussions with Westinghouse to build nuclear in their countries, given the benefits it provides to their energy systems. This follows earlier waves including railways, automobiles, electricity, telephones, computers, internet, and mobile devices. What is clear is that we are in the midst of a transformation of how we live and do business. What is never clear is exactly how and when this will affect people and businesses. There are many things that will not be recognizable in the future, and surely people will look back and find many of today’s activities quaint. This has been the case with all the above transformations, but the difference this time is that the speed and scale at which this is occurring are on another level. What is less clear today is who will make money, how they will make it, and whether we are building too fast. These questions pose the greatest investment opportunity and threat of our lifetimes. It is the sheer scale and speed at which this is happening that creates the challenge and opportunity. The large language model (LLM) companies have decided that they need to move fast or they will be left behind. Whether this is true is the $5-10 trillion question. One thing is very clear this time: access to capital will be the ultimate decider in this story, and those who have it will win. The good news is that this revolution is being led by companies that are as large as countries. This is very comforting, as these companies each have very profitable core businesses and are reinvesting profits from those businesses into this LLM race — and they can afford to do it. They may not like the outcome if it does not work out for them, but they will be fine financially. To remind you, many of the greatest businesses today went for years/decades without being profitable, but today they generate hundreds of billions in revenue. As an example, most television, radio, magazine, and other forms of advertising have transferred onto online platforms. This industry, which was worth zero at launch, has captured up to $5 trillion of value. At Brookfield, on a selective basis, this backdrop offers some outstanding opportunities to earn excellent returns without taking undue risk. Importantly, we approach these opportunities no differently than we have approached investments for decades. We focus on assets backed by long-term contracts with high-quality counterparties, where we take limited or no technology risk and can earn attractive risk-adjusted returns. We have decades of proven skills in building critical digital infrastructure, generating and connecting energy, raising large sums of money, and building relationships with the strongest in the world. As a result, our counterparty risk is very low when providing power, data centers, or compute to these technology titans, and the returns we are able to generate are good. This is possible because not many have our scale, not many can put together the capital required, and not many have the ability to get the assets built. We have all three. While many can commit capital to large projects, actually developing and building them is very difficult. It requires experience, operating expertise, and execution capability. We have spent decades building these capabilities across our businesses, and the combination of our investment expertise and operating capabilities allows us not only to identify and secure the best opportunities, but also to deliver them at scale. This has led, and continues to lead, to us being able to invest tens of billions into opportunities with very strong risk-adjusted returns. In our own businesses, we are also in the midst of deploying these LLM technologies to enhance revenues, reduce costs, scale businesses and transform manufacturing.
