Company’s Bylaws Provisions on the Exercise of Voting Rights Rules on the Exercise of Voting Rights Pursuant to Section 14 of the Company’s Bylaws, each common share shall be entitled to one vote in the resolution of the Meeting. Nevertheless, the Company’s Bylaws establish certain limits on the exercise of voting rights, as described below: (a) No shareholder, or Shareholder Group (as defined below), Brazilian or foreign, may cast votes in excess of five percent (5%) of the shares of the Company’s capital stock; and (b) The Foreign Shareholders (as defined below) and Foreign Shareholder Groups (as defined below) may not cast votes in excess of two thirds (2/3) of the total votes that may be exercised by the Brazilian Shareholders (as defined below) in attendance. The above limitations apply, jointly and successively, to Foreign Shareholders and Foreign Shareholder Groups. During the Meeting, the votes of Brazilian Shareholders and Foreign Shareholders shall be tallied separately for the item on the agenda. For this purpose, the Chairperson of the Meeting shall determine and communicate, upon the Meeting’s opening, the total number of votes that may be cast by the Brazilian Shareholders and by the Foreign Shareholders according to the voting limits described above. If the total number of votes of the Foreign Shareholders exceeds two thirds (2/3) of the votes that may be cast by the Brazilian Shareholders, the number of votes of each Foreign Shareholder, including those received by means of remote voting ballot (boletim de voto à distância) sent directly to the Company or through a third party, shall be proportionately reduced by the percentage of such excess, so that the total number of votes of the Foreign Shareholders does not exceed the limit of forty percent (40%) the votes that may be cast in the Meeting. The Company’s management shall not count votes in noncompliance with the Bylaws provisions described above. As for investment funds, only those with a common administrator whose investment and voting policy at general meetings, under the terms of the respective regulations, is the responsibility of the administrator, on a discretionary basis, are considered to be part of a Shareholder Group. Holders of securities issued under the Company’s Depositary Receipts program are not considered a Shareholder Group, unless they meet any of the criteria set forth in items (i) to (iv) described above. Any shareholders or Shareholder Groups that are represented by the same proxy, administrator or attorney in fact, shall be considered as members of the same Shareholder Group in the Meeting, except in the case of holders of securities issued in connection with the Company’s Depositary Receipts program, when represented by the respective Depositary Bank. In the event of shareholders’ agreements that govern the exercise of voting rights, all signatories thereto shall be considered members of the same Shareholder Group for purposes of the limitation on the number of votes described above, subject to the restriction of Section 17 of the Company’s Bylaws. “Foreign Shareholder Group” – A Shareholder Group shall be considered foreign whenever one or more of its members is a Foreign Shareholder. “Brazilian Shareholders” – The following are Brazilian Shareholders: (i) individuals born or naturalized in Brazil, residing in Brazil or abroad; (ii) legal entities organized under Brazilian private law that have their management based in Brazil, and which: a) have no foreign controlling shareholder or foreign parent company, unless the latter falls under item “b” of this definition; b) are controlled, directly or indirectly, by one or more individuals referred to in item (i) of this definition; and (iii) investment funds or clubs organized under the laws of Brazil and having their management based in Brazil and whose administrators and/ or majority unitholders are persons referred to in items (i) and (ii) of this definition. “Foreign Shareholders” - Foreign Shareholders are individuals, legal entities, investment funds or clubs and any other entities not included in the definition of Brazilian Shareholders, and those that fail to prove that they meet the requirements to be registered as Brazilian Shareholders. Pursuant to Section 20 of the Company’s Bylaws the persons present at the Meeting shall prove their status as a Brazilian Shareholder or a Foreign Shareholder by means of an identity document sent to the Company within forty-eight (48) hours prior to the Meeting. The LTI Plan is intended to promote: (i) the alignment of the interests of LTI Plan participants with those of the Company’s shareholders, fostering a long-term perspective, commitment to results, and the sustainable creation of value; and (ii) the attraction, retention, and engagement of professionals who are strategic to the Company and its controlled entities, in line with market best practices in corporate governance and executive compensation. Currently, the Company offers its executives and employees the opportunity to receive virtual shares (phantom shares) referenced to shares issued by the Company and settled exclusively in cash. While this variable compensation mechanism is already adopted by the Company, Management recognizes the importance of equity-based incentives as a tool to align interests, retain talent, and generate sustainable long-term value, while also enabling more efficient management of the accounting and cash costs associated with such incentives. Accordingly, and as a refinement of its compensation policy, Management proposes the approval of the LTI Plan as a multimodal plan, granting the Board of Directors the authority to determine, for each grant program established under the LTI Plan, the incentive modality, or combination of modalities, most appropriate among those provided for in the Plan for the respective participants, taking into consideration the Company’s strategic objectives, stage of development, and business needs. Management believes that the adoption of this structure is justified by the size, operational complexity, and strategic challenges inherent to a company of Embraer’s scale and business profile, whose planning horizon cannot be adequately addressed through a single incentive instrument. Under the LTI Plan, this delegation of authority to the Board of Directors is accompanied by clear and objective limits and guidelines, set forth in the LTI Plan itself as approved by shareholders, to which the Board of Directors shall be bound in exercising its authority. Such limits and guidelines include, among others: (i) the establishment of a maximum dilution limit, corresponding to the maximum number of shares that may be delivered under the Plan, expressed as a percentage of capital stock on a fully diluted basis; (ii) the ineligibility of members of the Board of Directors as beneficiaries of the LTI Plan; and (iii) the subjection of grants to minimum vesting periods and, depending on the type of grant, to performance indicators set forth in the LTI Plan. The adoption of a multimodal structure is based on the premise that each incentive modality produces distinct economic effects. Equity settled instruments, such as stock options and restricted shares, align participants with fluctuations in the Company’s share price and confer shareholder status, while cash-settled instruments, such as phantom shares, preserve the shareholder base from dilution but require cash disbursements by the Company. Likewise, 20 Manual and Management Proposal > Management Proposal awards subject to performance indicators emphasize performance, whereas awards subject solely to vesting conditions emphasize retention. This flexibility allows the Company to calibrate its incentives according to different participant profiles, business cycles, and market conditions on a global scale, combining modalities in order to balance the interests of shareholders, participants, and other stakeholders and to respond in a timely manner to new challenges and market developments over time, maintaining a competitive incentive policy. The LTI Plan has been structured with robust governance mechanisms designed to ensure alignment between compensation and sustainable shareholder value creation, which apply to all incentive modalities, regardless of the instrument selected by the Board of Directors for each program. Accordingly, the Plan includes, among other provisions, restrictions on hedging transactions, preventing participants from offsetting their exposure to share price fluctuations and thereby preserving the symmetry of risks between participants and shareholders, as well as clawback mechanisms, which allow for the recovery or cancellation of incentives under specific circumstances, discouraging excessive risk-taking and short-term gains at the expense of the Company. The LTI Plan also establishes objective criteria for the treatment of awards upon termination of employment or service, enhancing predictability, reducing discretion in their application, and reinforcing the link between incentives, retention, and effective contribution to the Company’s results. granted is exercised within parameters previously determined by shareholders. Consistent with corporate governance best practices, the LTI Plan provides that any change of control of the Company shall not trigger automatic accelerated vesting, and the original conditions established under the Plan shall remain in force. This approach seeks to prevent the acceleration of benefits unrelated to the actual satisfaction of applicable conditions and to preserve the impartiality of participants in evaluating potential transactions involving a change of control of the Company. Considering the Company’s strategic objectives and the characteristics of the LTI Plan, Management believes that its approval represents a natural evolution of the Company’s incentive policy and will strengthen its long-term incentive framework by combining flexibility in the selection of incentive instruments with the ability to adapt to different business cycles, while remaining subject to limits and safeguards established by shareholders themselves. Such framework is grounded in principles of meritocracy, alignment of interests with shareholders, and high standards of corporate governance. For these reasons, Management recommends the approval of the LTI Plan, as it believes that the Plan will contribute to the execution of the Company’s strategy, balance the interests of its various stakeholders, and support the sustainable creation of long-term value. In the event of death, the heirs, legal successors or estate administrator and, in the event of permanent disability, the Participant if legally competent or his or her curator, guardian, assistant, supporter (apoiador) or legal representative may exercise within 180 (one hundred eighty) days after the date of death or the declaration of permanent disability. After that period, unexercised Options and SARs shall be forfeited. If exercise has not occurred by the end of the period, the SARs shall be automatically settled by the Company in cash at the calculated amount for the benefit of the legal successors. (b) RSUs, PSUs, Phantom Shares and Matching Shares. Delivery of the Shares or payment in cash, as applicable, shall be made automatically by the Company within 90 (ninety) days after the specific court authorization for the estate administrator to take actions or the court approval of the legal successors, regardless of any request by the heirs. Any delay in obtaining the specific court authorization shall not extinguish the successors’ rights. Solely with respect to Incentives subject to performance conditions, the Board of Directors may, in its discretion and within that period, determine achievement of the Performance Factor at a higher level, taking into account the attainment level through the date of the Participant’s termination event. 19.3.-The Company may withhold from the amount to be delivered to the successors the taxes due as a result of receipt, subject to the provisions of Article VIII. 20.
