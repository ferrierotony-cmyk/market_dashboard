OUR BUSINESS AND STRATEGY West Fraser is a diversified wood products company with facilities in Canada, the United States, the United Kingdom, and Europe, manufacturing, marketing, selling, and distributing lumber, engineered wood products (OSB, LVL, MDF, plywood, particleboard), northern bleached softwood kraft pulp, paper, wood chips and other residuals. As at October 2, 2026, our business is comprised of 28 lumber mills, 15 OSB facilities, 3 plywood facilities, 3 MDF facilities, 1 particleboard facility, 1 LVL facility, 1 veneer facility, and 2 pulp and paper mills. Our goal at West Fraser is to generate strong financial results through the business cycle, supported by robust product and geographic diversity, and relying on our committed workforce, the quality of our assets and our well-established people and culture. This culture emphasizes cost control in all aspects of the business and operating in a sustainable, financially conservative and prudent manner. The North American wood products industry is cyclical and periodically faces difficult market conditions. Our earnings are sensitive to changes in world economic conditions, primarily those in North America, Asia and Europe and particularly to the U.S. housing market for new construction and repair and renovation spending. Most of our revenues are from sales of commodity products for which prices are sensitive to variations in supply and demand. As many of our costs are denominated in Canadian dollars, British pounds sterling and Euros, exchange rate fluctuations of the Canadian dollar, - 1 - British pound sterling and Euro against the United States dollar can and are anticipated to be a significant source of earnings volatility for us. We believe that maintaining a strong balance sheet and liquidity profile, along with our investment-grade issuer rating, enables us to execute a balanced capital allocation strategy. Our goal is to optimize our portfolio of assets as well as reinvest in our operations across all market cycles to strategically enhance productivity, product mix, and capacity and to maintain a leading cost position. We believe that a strong balance sheet also provides the financial flexibility to capitalize on growth opportunities, including the pursuit of opportunistic acquisitions and larger-scale strategic growth initiatives, and is a key tool in managing our business over the long term including returning capital to shareholders. RECENT DEVELOPMENTS Markets New home construction activity in the U.S. is a significant driver of lumber and OSB demand in North America. According to the U.S. Census Bureau, the seasonally adjusted annualized rate of U.S. housing starts was 1.22 million units in September 2026, with permits issued for 1.40 million units. In comparison, U.S. housing starts were 1.36 million units for the full year of 2025. New housing construction growth has been impacted by consumer confidence levels, elevated mortgage rates and housing affordability challenges. The conflict in the Middle East has added to consumer uncertainty and affordability challenges. A meaningful economic or employment slowdown, a prolonged higher-for-longer interest rate environment, or insufficient housing price adjustments could further constrain affordability and weigh on near-term demand for new home construction and our wood-based building products. Existing home sales remain historically low influenced by higher mortgage rates which have deterred consumers from refinancing as many hold lower rate long-term mortgages that were entered into prior to 2021. In addition, the U.S. central bank raised its key lending rate by 25 bps in September 2026. Long-term bond yields have also risen in recent months and remain elevated relative to recent years. These factors continue to influence mortgage rates and housing affordability, with 30 year mortgage rates exceeding 7%. Over the longer term, underlying household formation and improvements in housing affordability could provide support for U.S. housing demand. Federal measures to encourage housing supply may provide longer-term support for U.S. residential construction, although the timing and extent of any impact remain uncertain. In the third quarter, demand for our products used in repair and remodelling remained subdued. Prior signs of improving market conditions were tempered by the conflict in the Middle East and the associated rise in government bond yields. Historically low existing home sales combined with lower inflation-adjusted repair and renovation spending continues to weigh on overall activity levels. Over the medium to long term, the advanced age of the U.S. housing stock is expected to continue supporting renovation and repair activity and, consequently, demand for lumber, OSB and plywood. Continental lumber supply has trended lower in recent years due to mill capacity reductions across North America, with a significant number of these reductions announced as permanent. These capacity reductions have been attributed to several factors, including uneconomic fibre supply, mill modernization activity, the deterioration of residuals markets and increased duties and tariffs. While lumber supply rationalization and lower Canadian SPF imports into the U.S. have contributed to an improved U.S. market balance in 2026 relative to recent years, demand remains below historical levels and market conditions continue to be influenced by affordability challenges and elevated interest rates. Over the longer term, our ability to respond to a recovery in lumber demand may face challenges from timber supply limitations in key producing regions, the investment required for greenfield and brownfield project development, availability of cost-efficient labour, diminishing outlets for residuals and escalating capital costs. The North American OSB industry continues to operate in an oversupplied market environment. While recent OSB mill closures have removed some capacity from the market, announced greenfield and restart projects are expected to add notable capacity over the next several years, including projects currently under construction. The timing and pace at which this new capacity is absorbed will depend on the recovery of residential construction activity and broader demand fundamentals. Over the medium term, prolonged market oversupply and weaker economic returns could moderate the pace of additional industry investment beyond currently announced projects. According to industry data, from July 2024 through June 2026, approximately 21% of U.S. lumber consumption and 27% of U.S. OSB consumption was supplied by Canadian facilities. There has been a meaningful decline in Canadian lumber and wood-based building product imports into the U.S. over the last 4 years. Increased softwood lumber duties and the tariffs imposed by the U.S. administration’s Section 232 and Section 338 proclamations, along with loss of residual - 2 - markets, wildfires and fibre access constraints, have contributed to an environment of heightened financial uncertainty for Canadian-based manufacturers of these products. If Canadian exports to the U.S. are constrained because of these or any other factors, the supply of these products may fall short of U.S. demand levels over the near to medium term. Tariffs On July 20, 2026, the U.S. administration announced new tariffs of 50% on certain Canadian products imported into the U.S. under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 , effective August 22, 2026. On October 23, 2026, the U.S. administration announced modifications to the scope of certain tariffs imposed on Canadian products, effective October 30, 2026. The revised scope includes MDF products no thicker than 5 mm. In 2026, shipments to the United States of MDF products meeting the applicable criteria represented approximately 15% of our total MDF shipments. Our shipments to the U.S. of softwood lumber and OSB are not affected by Section 338 tariffs. Indefinite curtailment of two mills On October 28, 2026, we announced the indefinite curtailments of our Huguley oriented strand board (OSB) mill in Lanett, Alabama and our lumber mill in Leola, Arkansas. These curtailments are expected to take effect in the fourth quarter following orderly wind-downs. The indefinite curtailment of the Huguley OSB mill and the Leola lumber mill is the result of continued weakness in demand for wood products used in new home construction and repair and renovation applications. These actions are expected to reduce West Fraser's OSB capacity by 500 million square feet (3/8-inch) and SYP capacity by 225 million board feet, both on an annual basis. Over the medium to longer term, improvements in housing affordability supported by U.S. government policy changes, reductions in inflation and interest rates and the advanced age of the U.S. housing stock (with a median home age of approximately 45 years) are expected to support new home construction and repair and renovation activity that generates lumber, plywood and OSB demand. In addition, growing market penetration of mass timber in industrial and commercial applications is also expected to become a more significant source of demand growth for wood building products in North America. The seasonally adjusted annualized rate of U.S. housing starts was 1.22 million units in September 2026, with permits issued for 1.40 million units, according to the U.S. Census Bureau. While there are near-term uncertainties for new home construction and repair and renovation spending, owing in large part to weak consumer confidence and the level of mortgage rates and housing affordability, U.S. unemployment remains relatively low. However, U.S. employment growth has shown signs of slowing, while the conflict in the Middle East and the potential inflationary effects of tariff and other government policies may continue to exert upward pressure on inflation and interest rates. Given these developments, demand for our wood building products will continue to be challenged and may decline over the near term should the broader economy and employment slow or the trend in interest and mortgage rates continue to negatively impact consumer sentiment and housing affordability. We expect industry demand across Europe and the U.K. to remain broadly in line with current levels in the near term, as the market continues to deal with elevated interest rates, inflationary pressures brought on by the conflict in the Middle East and broader economic uncertainty. Over the longer term, we expect demand for our European products to increase as adoption of OSB and timber-frame construction expands and repair and renovation activity remains supported by an aging housing stock. We believe we are well positioned to navigate market conditions and capitalize on the long-term growth opportunities in our European markets. Softwood lumber dispute Canadian softwood lumber exports to the U.S. have been the subject of trade disputes and managed trade arrangements for several decades. The current round of countervailing and antidumping duties have been in place since April 2017, and we are required to make deposits in respect of these duties. Whether and to what extent we can realize a selling price to recover the impact of duties payable will largely depend on the strength of demand for softwood lumber. On October 14, 2026, the USDOC released final results for the AR7 POI, which disclosed a rate of 15.31% for CVD and 4.71% for ADD for West Fraser. Effective October 19, 2026, West Fraser’s cash deposit rate is 20.02%, which is a drop of 6.45% from the previous cash deposit rate of 26.47%. In addition, financial performance can be materially influenced by changes in product prices and the relative values of the Canadian and U.S. dollars. Our objective in managing capital is to ensure adequate liquidity and financial flexibility at all times, particularly at the lower points in the business cycle. Our main policy relating to capital management is to maintain a strong balance sheet and otherwise meet financial tests that rating agencies commonly apply for investment-grade issuers of public debt. We are currently rated as an investment grade issuer by two major rating agencies. We monitor and assess our financial performance to ensure that debt levels are prudent, taking into account the anticipated direction of the business cycle. When financing acquisitions, we combine cash on hand, debt, and equity financing in a proportion that is intended to maintain an investment-grade rating for debt throughout the cycle. Debt repayments are arranged, where possible, on a staggered basis that takes into account the uneven nature of anticipated cash flows. We have established committed revolving lines of credit that provide liquidity and flexibility when capital markets are restricted. In addition, as a normal part of our business, we have in the past and may from time to time seek to repurchase our outstanding securities through issuer bids or tender offers, open market purchases, privately negotiated transactions or otherwise. Such repurchases, if any, will depend on prevailing market conditions, our liquidity requirements, contractual and legal restrictions and other factors. A strong balance sheet and liquidity profile, along with our investment-grade issuer rating, are key elements of our goal to maintain a balanced capital allocation strategy. Priorities within this strategy include: reinvesting in our operations across all market cycles to strategically enhance productivity, product mix, and capacity; optimizing our portfolio of assets to reduce the variability of cash flows across market cycles; maintaining a leading cost position; maintaining financial flexibility to capitalize on growth opportunities, including the pursuit of acquisitions and larger-scale strategic growth initiatives; and returning capital to shareholders through dividends and share repurchases.
