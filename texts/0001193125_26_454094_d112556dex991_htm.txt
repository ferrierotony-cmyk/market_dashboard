OVERVIEW OF THE COMPANY’S BUSINESS OPERATIONS The Debtors, together with their Non-Debtor Affiliates, are a leading group of global forest products companies that process sustainably sourced wood into a wide range of products used primarily in paper, packaging, and construction applications. The Company’s principal executive office is in Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada and it has significant operations and assets in the United States, Canada, and Germany. Mercer’s shares of common stock are quoted and listed for trading on the NASDAQ Global Select Market under the symbol “MERC”. As described in greater detail below, the Company has two primary operating segments: the pulp segment (the “ Pulp Segment ”) and the solid wood segment (the “ Solid Wood Segment ”). Mercer is one of the world’s largest producers of “market” pulp and a growing leader in solid wood products. The Company’s large-scale, modern pulp mills produce northern bleached softwood kraft (“ NBSK ”) pulp, northern bleached hardwood kraft (“ NBHK ”) pulp, and biomass-based green electricity and bioextractives. Mercer also operates one of the world’s largest softwood lumber facilities and is firmly establishing itself as one of North America’s leading mass timber providers. In addition to traditional forest products, which are the core of its business, the Company generates green electricity using biofuels in connection with its pulping and sawmilling operations and sells surplus green electricity to third-party utilities and into regional electricity markets, thus providing the Company with a stable supplemental revenue source unrelated to pulp or lumber prices. 8 The following chart provides an overview of the Company’s global operations and geographic footprint: A. History and Formation Mercer was originally organized in 1968 as a business trust under the laws of the State of Washington. On March 1, 2006, Mercer converted from a business trust to a corporation organized under the laws of the State of Washington through a series of merger transactions. The Company’s principal executive offices are located in Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada. Mercer is the ultimate parent company of the Mercer International group and is the issuer of the Company’s publicly traded common stock and the Senior Notes. The Company’s operations are primarily carried out through Mercer’s various Non-Debtor Affiliates in the United States, Germany, and Canada. Mercer originally invested in various real estate assets with the intention of becoming a real estate investment trust. However, in 1985, the Company changed its strategic direction and began acquiring controlling interests in operating companies. In 1994, the Company entered the pulp business through the acquisition of the Rosenthal mill in Germany. Over the following decades, the Company expanded its pulp operations through a combination of organic investment and strategic acquisitions. In 1999, the Company converted the Rosenthal mill to the production of kraft pulp. The Company continued to expand its pulp operations with the construction of the Stendal mill in Germany, which commenced operations in 2004 following an investment of approximately $1.1 billion, and the subsequent acquisitions of the Celgar mill in British Columbia, Canada for approximately $210.0 million in 2005 and the Peace River mill in Alberta, Canada in 2018, as part of a share purchase transaction valued at approximately $344.0 million. Beginning in 2017, the Company expanded beyond its traditional pulp business into solid wood products with the acquisition of the Friesau mill in Germany. The Company subsequently expanded its solid wood business through the acquisition of the Spokane facility for approximately $51.3 million in 2021, the Torgau facility (through a share purchase transaction) for approximately $263.2 million in 2022, and the Conway facility and Okanagan facility from the Structurlam group of companies for approximately $82.1 million in 2023. 9 Throughout its history, the Company has pursued long-term growth in the forest products industry through a combination of strategic acquisitions and organic investments, including targeted capital expenditures to enhance and expand its existing assets. B. Current Business Operations i. Products & Services As described above, the Company’s two primary operating segments are the Pulp Segment and the Solid Wood Segment. Each segment is described in greater detail below. The following chart sets out the Company’s principal product and geographic sales mix for the six months ending June 30, 2026: (a) Pulp Segment The Pulp Segment consists of the manufacture, sale, and distribution of pulp, green electricity, and bio-chemicals at Mercer’s pulp mills located in Germany and Canada. The Company is one of the world’s largest producers of market NBSK pulp, a premium grade of pulp valued in part for its strength characteristics, which is sold primarily to tissue and paper producers. The Company operates four pulp mills, including two NBSK mills in Germany and two mills in Western Canada (one NBSK mill and one “swing” kraft mill capable of producing both NBSK and NBHK pulp). In addition to producing pulp, Mercer’s mills operate as large-scale bio-refineries that generate green electricity from biofuels produced during the pulp manufacturing process, including black liquor and wood waste. This energy is used to support mill operations, and surplus green electricity is sold to third-party utilities or into regional electricity markets. (b) Solid Wood Segment The Solid Wood Segment consists of the manufacture, sale, and distribution of lumber, engineered wood products, wood pallets, green electricity, biofuels, and wood residuals from the Company’s sawmills in Germany and its mass timber facilities in North America. The Company’s lumber products include dimensional, specialty, and other finished sawn lumber and are sold primarily to national and regional 10 distributors, construction firms, secondary manufacturers, retail yards, and home centers in the United States, Europe, and Asia. The Solid Wood Segment also involves the production of wood pallets, including Euro-pallets for customers primarily located in Europe. In addition, the Company manufactures engineered wood products, including cross-laminated timber (“ CLT ”), glulam, and finger joint lumber. CLT and glulam are used in residential and commercial construction, with CLT serving as a more sustainable alternative to traditional building materials, such as steel and concrete. Wood residuals generated through the manufacturing process are used internally, sold to third parties, or converted into products such as wood pellets and briquettes, while certain facilities also generate renewable electricity from biomass. ii. KEY EVENTS LEADING TO COMMENCEMENT OF CHAPTER 11 CASES A. Challenges Facing the Company ’ s Business Although the Company remains a leading global forest products producer, the Debtors are confronting acute financial challenges due to a confluence of factors, including among others: (i) an unsustainable capital structure driven by funding to support capacity expansion initiatives, (ii) significant near-term debt maturities, which would be challenging to refinance or extend absent the Restructuring, (iii) ongoing geopolitical developments, including evolving global trade and tariff dynamics, (iv) sluggish global economic growth, and (v) financial challenges, driven by substantial margin compression driven by rising input costs and adverse market conditions, including lower market prices for pulp. Each of these factors is described in greater detail below. Despite the Company’s recent efforts to address these challenges, including through negotiations with the lenders under the Canadian ABL Facility and the German RCF, cost-saving measures, liquidity-preservation initiatives, and other actions, the Company ultimately determined that a comprehensive financial restructuring through the Chapter 11 Cases was necessary to reduce its funded debt burden and associated interest expense, enhance liquidity, strengthen its balance sheet, address significant near-term debt maturities, and position the Company for greater financial stability on a go-forward basis. i. Capacity Expansion Initiatives and Increased Debt Financing In recent years, the Company has made substantial investments to increase its production capacity and broaden its product offerings. The Company began expanding beyond its traditional pulp business before the COVID-19 pandemic and accelerated that strategy beginning in 2021, particularly through investments in solid wood and mass timber. This period began amid strong demand and pricing in the U.S. and European lumber markets, including strong demand from the U.S. housing and home renovation markets and steady demand in Europe. From 2021 through 2023 alone, Mercer invested approximately $396.6 million in acquisitions to expand its solid wood operations and product mix. While these investments expanded the scale and breadth of the Company’s operations, they also required substantial capital and contributed to increased leverage and financial obligations, placing additional strain on the Company’s liquidity and overall financial position. • Friesau Mill . In April 2017, Mercer entered the solid wood business through its acquisition of the Friesau mill in Germany for approximately $61.6 million. The Friesau mill is a large-scale sawmill that produces lumber for customers in Europe, the United States, and other international markets. The acquisition represented a significant expansion of Mercer’s business beyond its traditional pulp operations. • Peace River Mill . Near-Term Debt Maturities The Company faces near-term maturities under two of its principal secured credit facilities. The Canadian ABL Facility matures in January 2027, and the German RCF matures in September 2027. Without the Restructuring, the Company would not have sufficient liquidity to repay these facilities at maturity and, despite carefully evaluating potential financing alternatives, would not be able to refinance or extend both of these facilities on commercially viable terms. The need to address these maturities was therefore a significant factor in the timing of the Restructuring and the commencement of the Chapter 11 Cases. As described above, the Restructuring will allow the Company to refinance or otherwise address these facilities before their respective maturities as part of a broader series of transactions designed to maximize going-concern value for the benefit of the Company’s stakeholders. iii. Macroeconomic and Geopolitical Factors Mercer operates in highly cyclical industries in which the prices of pulp, lumber, and other forest products are driven largely by global supply and demand. In recent periods, continued economic uncertainty and weak conditions in key markets, elevated interest rates, and geopolitical uncertainty have contributed to challenging demand conditions for many of the Company’s products. At the same time, Mercer has 19 experienced significant increases in wood fiber costs and higher transportation costs, together with adverse foreign exchange effects and other cost pressures. The combination of weak demand, lower NBSK pulp pricing, elevated interest rates and input costs, and adverse foreign exchange movements has contributed to significant margin compression and placed pressure on the Company’s earnings, cash flows, and debt service capacity. • Weaker Demand and Pulp Pricing . Market conditions for pulp have remained challenging, particularly for NBSK pulp in China and North America. Demand in China, one of the world’s largest pulp markets, has been constrained by an oversupplied paper market and broader economic and trade uncertainty. During the first half of 2026, quoted NBSK net prices in China averaged approximately $672 per tonne, compared with approximately $764 per tonne during the same period in 2025, while quoted NBSK list prices in North America also declined materially. Mercer’s average NBSK sales realizations decreased approximately 11% during the first half of 2026 compared with the prior-year period. Demand for solid wood products in Europe has also remained subdued due to weak economic conditions. Although Solid Wood Segment revenues increased in the first half of 2026 and average lumber sales realizations increased by approximately 5%, lumber revenues declined due to lower sales volumes, and higher fiber costs more than offset the benefit of higher sales realizations, placing additional pressure on segment profitability. In the Pulp Segment, weaker demand has reduced the prices Mercer receives for its NBSK pulp at the same time that the Company is facing elevated production costs, placing significant pressure on margins and cash flows. • Higher Fiber and Other Input Costs . The Company has also experienced substantial increases in the cost of wood fiber, one of its most significant production inputs. These pressures have been particularly acute in Germany, where reduced fiber availability and increased demand for wood as an energy source, including in connection with ongoing geopolitical conflicts, have contributed to higher fiber costs. During the first half of 2026, Mercer’s per-unit pulp fiber costs increased approximately 18% compared with the same period in 2025, primarily due to reduced supply in Germany and Canada and strong demand for wood as an energy source in Germany. Fiber costs represented approximately 85% of lumber cash production costs during the first half of 2026, and per-unit fiber costs for lumber production increased approximately 32% year over year. High fiber costs also caused the Company to reduce production at its German pulp mills. As a result, the Company has faced higher production costs and, in certain cases, reduced production volumes. • Geopolitical and Energy Cost Pressures. Ongoing geopolitical conflicts, including the conflicts in Ukraine and the Middle East, have increased economic uncertainty and have generally disrupted global energy markets. The conflict in the Middle East, in particular, has driven oil and other energy prices higher, adding inflationary pressure to freight, fiber, and other production costs. At the same time, heightened geopolitical uncertainty has weighed on macroeconomic activity, contributing to weaker demand for the Company’s products. These conditions have contributed to higher input costs at a time of weaker demand and pricing, thus putting further pressure on margins and cash flows. • Foreign Exchange Volatility . Mercer’s global geographic footprint also exposes the Company to foreign exchange risk. Most of the operating costs of the Company’s German mills are incurred in euros and most of the operating costs of its Canadian mills are incurred in Canadian dollars, while the majority of the Company’s products are priced in U.S. dollars. As a result, a weakening U.S. dollar relative to the euro or Canadian dollar can increase the Company’s costs in U.S. dollar terms and place pressure on operating margins and cash flows. During the first half of 2026, Mercer experienced an approximately $15.7 million negative foreign exchange impact on operating loss compared with the same period in 2025, primarily as a result of the weaker U.S. dollar increasing its euro- and Canadian-dollar-denominated costs and expenses. 20 Collectively, these conditions have contributed to weaker demand across the Company’s key markets and significant margin compression notwithstanding the Company’s continued efforts to reduce costs and improve operational efficiency. Mercer’s Operating EBITDA declined from approximately $243.7 million in 2024 to negative $22.2 million in 2025 and was negative $13.1 million during the first half of 2026, compared with positive $26.5 million during the same period in 2025. During the first half of 2026, the Company also incurred approximately $51.0 million of inventory impairment charges, primarily against pulp and fiber inventory as a result of low pulp prices and high fiber costs. The Company also used approximately $100.8 million of cash in operating activities during the first half of 2026, compared with approximately $7.5 million during the same period in 2025. Against the backdrop of the Company’s increased indebtedness described above, these macroeconomic and geopolitical factors have placed additional strain on the Company’s capital structure and liquidity. iv. Financial Challenges As of the Petition Date, the Company had approximately $1.6 billion of funded debt and in the first half of 2026, the Company had generated negative Operating EBITDA of approximately $13.1 million. In light of the financial and operating pressures described above, the Company determined that its existing capital structure was not sustainable over the long term. Under the prepetition capital structure, the Company’s annual cash interest expense was approximately $122 million. The approaching debt maturities described above further constrained the Company’s liquidity and shortened the timeframe available to address its challenges. Through the Chapter 11 Cases and the Restructuring, the Debtors seek to reduce their aggregate funded debt by approximately $900.0 million, address the Company’s near-term debt maturities, reduce annual cash interest expense by approximately $100.0 million, and position the Company with a stronger balance sheet and improved liquidity profile on a go-forward basis.
