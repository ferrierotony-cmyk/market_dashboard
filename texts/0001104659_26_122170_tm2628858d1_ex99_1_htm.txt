(NYSE: RWT; “Redwood”, “we”, “us” or the “Company”), a leader in expanding access to housing for homebuyers and renters, today announced that its Board of Directors (the “Board”) has unanimously approved a plan to discontinue the Company’s election to be treated as a real estate investment trust for U.S. federal income tax purposes (“REIT”) and convert to a taxable C-corporation, effective for the calendar year ending December 31, 2027 (the “C-corporation Conversion”). Redwood also today announced plans for a preferred-for-common stock exchange offer designed to provide existing common stockholders, particularly those who prioritize current income, with an alternative way to participate in the Company's future (the “Exchange Offer”). “Over the past year, and in light of the Company’s strategic transformation, the Board and management team have been actively reviewing Redwood’s organizational structure, balance sheet, capital allocation priorities and long-term strategic objectives with the assistance of independent financial and legal advisors,” said Greg H. Kubicek, Chair of the Board. “Today’s actions represent an important next step to enhance value and lower our cost of capital as part of the continued evolution of Redwood’s business model.” C-Corporation Conversion Plan The C-corporation Conversion plan is expected to deliver three principal economic benefits: · Greater flexibility to retain and reinvest earnings in the Company’s mortgage banking platforms. As part of the C-corporation Conversion plan, the Board is realigning the Company’s quarterly dividend policy to the level of $0.02 per common share – a level consistent with banks, asset managers and specialty finance companies with comparable operating models – allowing the Company to retain and reinvest a greater portion of its earnings. The new quarterly dividend policy is expected to become effective upon the Board’s declaration of the fourth quarter 2026 common stock dividend in December 2026. · A pathway to a more efficient financing structure and a lower cost of capital. A C-corporation structure, supported by greater earnings retention and a stronger balance sheet, will broaden the Company’s access to institutional unsecured debt and equity capital and provide greater flexibility in future financing arrangements. · More effective utilization of Redwood’s existing tax assets and lower tax liabilities in the near term. The C-corporation Conversion will allow the Company to use net operating loss carryforwards (“NOLs”) from its REIT-qualified businesses to offset taxes on earnings from its tax-paying businesses, which represent the vast majority of total earnings, effectively lowering the tax liabilities, and related cash expense, of the Company’s mortgage banking businesses over a multi-year period. Because of the utility the tax assets will have in this new structure, the Company expects to recognize an initial benefit to book value in the fourth quarter of 2026 of approximately $0.73 to $0.77 per share (estimated benefit as of September 30, 2026). “Redwood has evolved into a premier mortgage credit platform, sourcing, aggregating and managing residential mortgage assets for a growing network of institutional capital partners,” said Christopher J. Abate, Chief Executive Officer of Redwood. “Our next chapter is about translating those competitive advantages into greater value for shareholders. With a new corporate structure and resilient core business, we are confident in our ability to lower our cost of capital, deepen access to accretive sources of financing, reduce leverage, compound earnings and book value, and create lasting value for shareholders.” Mr. Abate continued, “Our earnings announcement today noted that we have upsized our corporate secured financing facility and extended its maturity through March 2030, further strengthening our financing flexibility as we begin this new chapter.” Exchange Offer Planned to Provide Income-Oriented Common Stockholders with a High Dividend Preferred Stock Investment Option The Company plans to launch the Exchange Offer during the week of November 9, 2026. The Exchange Offer will provide common stockholders the opportunity to exchange some or all of the Company common stock they hold for shares of a new issue of 11.00% Series B Preferred Stock of the Company (the “Series B Preferred Stock”). The Company currently plans to exchange $50 million of common stock for shares of Series B Preferred Stock, and may increase the size of the Exchange Offer if it is meaningfully oversubscribed. · The Exchange Offer is intended to provide common stockholders seeking a high level of continuing dividend income with an opportunity to exchange the shares of common stock they hold for shares of Series B Preferred Stock, which offer both dividend priority and seniority over common stock in Redwood's capital structure. · The Exchange Offer will be conducted through a modified Dutch auction, with the auction results determining the final exchange ratio of Series B Preferred Stock for Company common stock. We anticipate that exchanging common stockholders will receive Series B Preferred Stock with a face value greater than (i.e., a premium to) the value of the common stock exchanged. · The Series B Preferred Stock will have an 11% coupon rate, with the effective yield potentially enhanced by the final exchange ratio in the Exchange Offer. · The Company intends to apply to list the Series B Preferred Stock on the New York Stock Exchange. Mr. Abate continued, “We know that some of our shareholders value the dividend that Redwood pays and we wanted to ensure they have an option to remain invested in the Company through our C-corporation transition process and continue to receive a high dividend. The Exchange Offer is designed to provide exchanging shareholders a preferred security with an effective yield of at least 11%, while allowing other common shareholders to remain fully invested in the growth of our platform and enterprise and benefit from the book value and earnings per share accretion that would result from this Exchange Offer.” The Exchange Offer has not commenced. The final terms of the Exchange Offer will be announced at the formal launch of the Exchange Offer. Protecting the Value of Redwood's Tax Assets As part of the C-corporation Conversion, today the Board unanimously adopted a Tax Benefits Preservation Rights Plan (the “Rights Plan”) to help protect the Company’s ability to use its NOLs and other tax assets under Section 382 of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as amended (the “IRC”). Redwood estimates that it currently has approximately $304 million of U.S. federal NOLs available to offset future taxable income following the C-corporation Conversion. The Company’s ability to use these NOLs would be substantially limited if the Company experienced an “ownership change” within the meaning of Section 382 of the IRC. The Board adopted the Rights Plan to help ensure that the NOLs are not impaired by changes to the Company’s stockholder base. Under the Rights Plan, the rights generally become exercisable only if a person or group (an “acquiring person”) acquires ownership of 4.9% or more of Company common stock and certain other Company securities (the “Specified Threshold”), subject to certain exceptions. In that situation, each holder of a right (other than the acquiring person, whose rights will become void and will not be exercisable) will be entitled to purchase, at the then-current exercise price, shares of Company common stock at a 50% discount. The Board, at its option, may exchange each right (other than rights owned by the acquiring person that have become void) in whole or in part, at an exchange ratio of one share of Company common stock per outstanding right, subject to adjustment. If a person or group’s ownership equals or exceeds the Specified Threshold at the time of today’s announcement of the Rights Plan, then that person’s or group’s existing ownership will be grandfathered. Grandfathered stockholders that hold in excess of 5.0% are permitted to acquire up to an additional 0.5%. In addition to the Rights Plan, our charter prohibits any person or group from acquiring “beneficial ownership” of our “capital stock” (as those terms are defined in our charter) in excess of 9.8% of our outstanding capital stock, absent a written waiver from the Board. In order to further help protect the Company’s tax assets in connection with the planned Exchange Offer, the Company intends to incorporate restrictions on the level of ownership of the Series B Preferred Stock issued in the Exchange Offer, which will be disclosed at the formal launch of the Exchange Offer. Additional information regarding the Rights Plan is contained in a Current Report on Form 8-K filed by the Company today with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (the “SEC”). Positioning Redwood's Common Stock for a Broader Investor Base As part of the C-corporation Conversion, the Board has also unanimously approved a one-for-five reverse stock split (the “Reverse Stock Split”) of the Company’s common stock, which is scheduled to be effective at market open on January 4, 2027. The Reverse Stock Split is being effected to enhance the marketability of the Company’s common stock, broaden the Company’s appeal to investors and better align its share structure with Redwood's evolving business model. Additional information about the Reverse Stock Split is contained in a Current Report on Form 8-K filed by the Company today with the SEC.
