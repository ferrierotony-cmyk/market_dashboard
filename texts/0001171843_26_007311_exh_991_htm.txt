We look forward to continuing this momentum in Q4 and beyond.” LNP Litigation Arbutus today announced that the Company and Genevant have filed additional lawsuits in Japan seeking to enforce patents protecting their innovative lipid nanoparticle (“LNP”) technology against Pfizer Japan Inc., as well as an amendment of a previously filed lawsuit in Canada against Pfizer, BioNTech and certain of their affiliates (together “Pfizer/BioNTech”) to include infringement of a newly issued LNP patent. Arbutus and Genevant are seeking monetary relief, as well as injunctions against Pfizer/BioNTech’s mRNA-LNP COVID-19 vaccines and any other products that infringe the asserted patents. In one lawsuit filed in Japan, which provides for submission of evidence at the time of case filing, Arbutus and Genevant submitted testing results of commercial Pfizer/BioNTech product samples sourced from the U.S. and Europe which establish that the samples contain LNPs falling under the protective scope of the claims of the Company’s lipid composition patent. The newly filed cases are: Japan: Tokyo District Court Case seeking a permanent injunction and damages for infringement of Japanese Patent No. 5,475,753. Japan: Tokyo District Court Case seeking a permanent injunction and damages for infringement of Japanese Patent No. 7,936,499. Additionally, the previously filed Canadian case (Federal Court of Canada File No. T-3200-26) was amended to add claims for Pfizer/BioNTech’s infringement of newly issued Canadian Patent No. 2,767,127. In July 2026, Arbutus, along with Genevant, filed three international lawsuits against Pfizer/BioNTech seeking to enforce patents protecting the Company’s patented LNP technology across 21 countries. In March 2026, Arbutus, along with its exclusive licensee, Genevant Sciences (“Genevant”), entered into a settlement agreement to resolve all global patent infringement litigation and patent revocation proceedings involving Moderna (the “Moderna Settlement Agreement”). As part of the settlement, Moderna paid Arbutus and Genevant $950 million in July 2026 (the “Noncontingent Settlement Payment”) and will pay an additional $1.3 billion contingent upon an appellate ruling that 28 U.S.C. §1498 does not bar Arbutus’ and Genevant’s claims against Moderna for patent infringement, except as to doses characterized by the district court as having gone to U.S. government employees (the “Moderna §1498 Appeal”). In July 2026, the Company received $178.4 million as its share of the Noncontingent Settlement Payment, which includes reimbursement of the Company’s litigation costs. On November 5, 2026, expedited oral argument was held at the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit regarding the Moderna §1498 Appeal, and a decision is forthcoming. For more information about the terms and conditions of the settlement with Moderna, including the contingent payment, please refer to Arbutus’ Quarterly Report on Form 10-Q to be filed with the SEC on November 10, 2026 and Annual Report on Form 10-K filed with the SEC on March 23, 2026. Corporate Updates In early October 2026, the Company successfully completed its modified “Dutch Auction” tender offer (the “Offer”), returning $230 million of capital to shareholders. The Offer followed the Company’s receipt of $178.4 million in July 2026 as its share of the Noncontingent Settlement Payment and a cash dividend of $67.3 million from Genevant in September 2026, which represented a distribution of Genevant’s earnings in connection with the Noncontingent Settlement Payment. In the Offer, the Company purchased 46 million common shares for cancellation at $5.00 per share, representing approximately 23% of its issued and outstanding common shares as of September 29, 2026. In October 2026, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (“FDA”) lifted the clinical hold on the Investigational New Drug (“IND”) application for AB-101, allowing the Company to engage in additional clinical development of AB-101 in the United States. Arbutus is evaluating next steps for the continued development of AB-101. Financial Results Cash, Cash Equivalents and Investments As of September 30, 2026, the Company had cash, cash equivalents and investments in marketable securities of $337.5 million compared to $91.5 million as of December 31, 2025. The Company returned $230 million of capital to shareholders in early October 2026 in connection with the completion of the Offer. During the nine months ended September 30, 2026, the Company received $225.7 million of net cash from operating activities and received $19.3 million of proceeds from the exercise of stock options. Revenue Total revenue was $0.3 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, compared to $0.5 million for the same period in 2025. The decrease of $0.2 million was due primarily to a decrease in license royalty revenues, primarily due to a decline in Alnylam’s sales of ONPATTRO. Operating Expenses Research and development expenses were $2.6 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, compared to $5.8 million for the same period in 2025. The decrease of $3.2 million was due primarily to cost savings from the Company’s decisions to reduce its workforce and discontinue in-house scientific research, as well as lower clinical trial costs as studies neared completion. General and administrative expenses were $8.0 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, compared to $3.0 million for the same period in 2025. This increase was due primarily to higher employee compensation-related expenses driven by the one-time cash bonuses paid to the Company’s executives in consideration of the significant efforts and accomplishments associated with Moderna Settlement Agreement. There were no restructuring costs in the quarter ended September 30, 2026, compared to $0.1 million for the same period in 2025. Net Income/Loss For the quarter ended September 30, 2026, the Company’s net income was $59.1 million, or net income of $0.30 per basic and diluted common share, as compared to a net loss of $7.7 million, or a net loss of $0.04 per basic and diluted common share, for the quarter ended September 30, 2025. Outstanding Shares As of September 30, 2026, the Company had 199.3 million common shares issued and outstanding, as well as 6.4 million stock options and unvested restricted stock units outstanding. (AB-729) Imdusiran is an RNAi therapeutic specifically designed to reduce all hepatitis B viral proteins and antigens, including hepatitis B surface antigen (“HBsAg”), which is thought to be a key prerequisite to enable reawakening of a patient’s immune system to control the virus. Imdusiran targets hepatocytes using Arbutus’ novel covalently conjugated N -Acetylgalactosamine delivery technology enabling subcutaneous delivery. In Arbutus’ Phase 2a clinical trials, eight patients with chronic hepatitis B (“cHBV”) achieved functional cure following treatment with imdusiran and nucleos(t)ide analogue (“NA”) therapy in combination with either pegylated interferon alfa-2a or low dose nivolumab plus an immunotherapeutic. To date, all eight patients remain virally suppressed and off NA therapy, with five out of the eight patients continuing to sustain functional cure for over two years. An additional 41 patients across the Company’s Phase 2a clinical trials were able to remain off NA therapy for at least 48 weeks during their Phase 2a clinical trials following treatment with imdusiran. Two additional patients who discontinued NA therapy in their Phase 2a clinical trials have now achieved functional cure during their participation in long-term follow-up. Functional cure is defined as sustained HBsAg seroclearance and hepatitis B virus deoxyribonucleic acid (“HBV DNA”) less than the lower limit of quantification after 24 weeks off treatment, with or without anti-hepatitis B surface antibodies. Clinical data generated thus far has shown imdusiran to be generally safe and well-tolerated, while also providing meaningful reductions in HBsAg and HBV DNA. About AB-101 AB-101 is an oral PD-L1 inhibitor candidate that is designed to allow for controlled checkpoint blockade while minimizing the systemic safety issues typically seen with checkpoint antibody therapies. Immune checkpoints such as PD-1/PD-L1 play an important role in the induction and maintenance of immune tolerance and in T-cell activation, for example against hepatitis B virus (“HBV”). In Arbutus’ Phase 1a/1b clinical trial, AB-101 was generally well-tolerated with evidence of high receptor occupancy. About HBV Hepatitis B is a potentially life-threatening liver infection caused by HBV. HBV can cause chronic infection which leads to a higher risk of death from cirrhosis and liver cancer. cHBV infection represents a significant unmet medical need. The World Health Organization estimates that over 250 million people worldwide suffer from cHBV infection, while other estimates indicate that approximately 2 million people in the United States suffer from cHBV infection. Approximately 1.1 million people die every year from complications related to cHBV infection despite the availability of effective vaccines and current treatment options.
