EX-99.1 2 ex99-1.htm EX-99.1 Nexa Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results including Net Loss of US$47 million and Adjusted EBITDA of US$301 million Luxembourg, October 29, 2026 – Nexa Resources S.A. (“Nexa Resources”, “Nexa”, or the “Company”) announces today its results for the three and nine-month periods ended on September 30, 2026. This Earnings Release should be read in conjunction with the “Condensed consolidated interim financial statements (unaudited) at and for the three and nine-month periods ended on September 30, 2026” (“Financial Statements”). This document contains forward-looking statements. CEO Message – Ignacio Rosado The third quarter marked another step forward in our performance. Adjusted EBITDA reached US$301 million, up 62% year-over-year, with the Adjusted EBITDA margin expanding 7.7 percentage points to 32.1%. Year-to-date, Adjusted EBITDA totaled US$869 million, an increase of 84%. We reported a net loss of US$47 million, including a US$124 million non-cash impairment charge related to our Brazilian smelting assets and a US$51 million silver-streaming remeasurement adjustment, which did not involve an outflow of cash in the quarter. We generated US$185 million in net cash from operating activities and US$73 million in free cash flow. Net leverage declined to 1.24x from 1.40x in June, primarily reflecting higher LTM Adjusted EBITDA. Net debt decreased by approximately US$21 million, while gross debt remained broadly unchanged, while maintaining solid liquidity. Mining zinc production rose 7% quarter-over-quarter to 85kt, driven by Aripuanã, where the fourth tailings filter lifted average plant utilization to 84% from 71% in 2Q26, reaching 90% in September. Smelting production increased 28% to 159kt, with Cajamarquilla operating at full capacity and processing most of the cathode inventory accumulated after the May fire incident. Smelting sales volumes increased 9% to 146kt, and our 2026 sales guidance remains unchanged. The zinc concentrate market remained tight, with spot treatment charges still in negative territory, a challenging environment for non-integrated smelters. Our integrated business model allows us to capture value across the chain: higher zinc prices supported mining results, while stronger contributions from by-products sustained our smelting margins. We revised our 2026 CapEx guidance upward by 10% to US$420 million, mainly to accelerate mine development at Aripuanã to support higher throughput in 2027, to bring forward investments in the Cerro Pasco Integration Project, and to reflect the appreciation of the Brazilian real. We also updated our cost guidance, lowering the mining cash cost range due to stronger by-product credits and raising the smelting cash cost range to reflect higher zinc prices and the stronger Brazilian real. Safety remains a priority for us, our injury frequency rate improved in the quarter and there were no fatalities. We are preparing our operations for the 2026-2027 El Niño event, which official forecasts indicate could reach strong to very strong intensity. There was no impact in the quarter. As part of our preparedness efforts, we built strategic zinc concentrate inventories to support smelter continuity under different scenarios, and our broader measures are outlined in the Sustainability Highlights section. In August, Votorantim signed a definitive agreement to sell its controlling stake in Nexa to Boliden. We continue to work constructively with all parties toward Closing, expected in the first quarter of 2027, subject to customary approvals. Until Closing, Nexa will continue to operate as a separate company, with no changes to our strategy, capital allocation priorities, or commitments to stakeholders. As we monitor political and macroeconomic developments in our key markets, including trade policy, our priorities remain clear: operating safely, maximizing free cash flow, reducing gross debt, and creating long-term value for our shareholders and the communities where we operate. Across the Company's mining and smelting operations, safety culture underpins the continuity of production, while the strength of community relationships directly affects permitting timelines and project execution. Safety: lower injury frequency in the quarter; new controls at the Pasco Complex Performance § In 3Q26, the total recordable injury frequency rate (“TRIFR”) improved to 1.34 from 1.50 in 3Q25, with fewer recordable injuries despite approximately 19% more hours worked. Nexa recorded no fatalities. Year-to-date, TRIFR stood at 2.06, compared with 1.72 in 9M25. To reverse this trend, Nexa is reinforcing controls on critical risks, including the fatigue and line-of-fire initiatives described below. Fire events § Cajamarquilla: Operations have been running at or above normal levels since June. The related insurance claim remains under assessment, and in 3Q26 Nexa received an advance payment of US$1.5 million under the claim. For further details, refer to Cajamarquilla under the Smelting Operational Performance section. § Três Marias: the action plan established following the fire event was completed, with all planned actions executed. Implementation was monitored monthly by senior management. Fatigue and line-of-fire risk § In August and September 2026, Nexa began installing fatigue and drowsiness monitoring cabins at the Cerro Pasco Complex in Peru to identify risk early and prevent incidents. As of October 26 th , 5 cabins have been installed, covering 95% of operators. 715 contractor employees were trained in fatigue and drowsiness management. § In August 2026, Nexa launched the “Line of Fire” campaign at El Porvenir and Atacocha, addressing entrapment, crushing, impact and unexpected energy release risks. Protective equipment designed for a more diverse workforce § In August 2026, Nexa completed the proof of concept of a safety helmet designed to fit a wider range of hair types, textures and styles and to meet the same safety requirements as the standard model. In an assessment with more than 40 employees, comfort and fit approval reached 95% for the prototype, compared with 60% for the standard helmet. The next stage includes technical refinements, safety certification and industrialization planning, expected to be completed by 1Q27, subject to successful validation. Resource efficiency: lower cement use at Cerro Lindo, added filtration capacity at Aripuanã Backfill optimization at Cerro Lindo § In 9M26, cement consumption at Cerro Lindo’s backfill plant was approximately 20% below budgeted levels, supported by the backfill optimization project commissioned in March 2026, which uses additives to reduce cement requirements. Dry-stack tailings capacity at Aripuanã § Commissioned in 2Q26, the fourth tailings filter removed the main constraint on Aripuanã’s processing capacity and expanded the site’s ability to filter and dry-stack its tailings, a method that avoids conventional tailings dams and allows water to be recovered and reused in the process. In 3Q26, average plant utilization reached 84%, increasing to 90% in September. For further details, refer to Aripuanã under the Mining Operational Performance section. Circular economy: new uses for jarosite, Waelz aggregate and pyrite Jarosite § At Juiz de Fora, Nexa is preparing to implement a 1-tonne-per-hour pilot plant for Geojar, a jarosite-based additive developed to replace lime or cement in the stabilization of tailings for dry stacking. In tests under relevant operating conditions, results indicate the additive could reduce the time required for tailings to reach compaction conditions from approximately 3.5 days to 0.5 day. Nexa has also submitted a proposal to FINEP, Brazil’s public innovation funding agency, to support a future industrial-scale unit. -- 7 -- Table of Contents Waelz aggregate § At Juiz de Fora, Nexa has supplied approximately 3,000 tonnes of Waelz aggregate, a by-product of the smelter’s Waelz kiln, for road paving in partnership with local municipalities, replacing virgin crushed stone and avoiding an estimated 44 tCO₂e. Municipalities have formally requested approximately 10,000 tonnes to date, around 30% of annual generation. Pyrite § At Cerro Lindo, Nexa completed pyrite concentration tests using tailings to assess product quality and its suitability for logistics and commercialization, and two potential buyers have formally expressed interest in the material. The next step is an engineering study to define the minimum investment required, leveraging existing infrastructure. Climate: El Niño preparedness § Nexa is closely monitoring the 2026-2027 El Niño and Coastal El Niño events. Official forecasts from agencies including NOAA, INMET, and SENAMHI indicate the potential for strong conditions, with NOAA projecting strong to very strong conditions through the first quarter of 2027. In 3Q26, there were no operational impacts related to these phenomena. § To strengthen its preparedness, the Company engaged a specialized climate-risk consultancy to assess potential impacts on its operations in Brazil and Peru, covering hazards such as droughts, wildfires, flooding, landslides and heat waves. The assessment is being updated to incorporate the latest forecasts. Based on the assessments’ findings, Nexa reviewed its preparedness and response measures across operating units and corporate functions. Effects are expected to vary by region and over time: Brazil § Although rainfall in the Southeast and Central-West has recently been above average, supporting the recovery of hydro reservoirs, the climate-risk assessment indicates a risk of drier and hotter conditions over the coming months, with water availability and wildfires as the main risk factors to watch. Nexa is advancing water management measures at Vazante and strengthening wildfire prevention and emergency preparedness at Vazante and Aripuanã, including firebreak maintenance, vegetation clearance, firefighting equipment readiness and contingency planning, as well as measures to protect workers from heat exposure. Peru § SENAMHI’s September 2026 outlook indicates above-normal rainfall as the most likely scenario for Peru’s central coast, where Cajamarquilla is located, mainly from November to January, and normal to above-normal rainfall in the western central highlands, including the Cerro Lindo area, through February 2027. This increases exposure to flooding and landslides at these sites. For the Pasco highland operations (Atacocha and El Porvenir), the outlook indicates normal to below-normal rainfall, although localized extreme events cannot be ruled out. Response measures focus on operational continuity and preparedness for extreme rainfall, including increasing inventories of critical materials, building strategic zinc concentrate inventories to support smelter continuity, expanding storage capacity and transportation alternatives for concentrates, calcine and sulfuric acid, maintaining drainage systems, conducting preventive geotechnical inspections and monitoring areas exposed to flooding and landslides, and coordinating with local authorities and communities. Energy § Nexa does not expect its energy costs to be materially affected during the event. In Peru, 100% of the Company’s energy requirements are contracted, including for 2027, which shields it from higher spot prices. In Brazil, Nexa’s uncovered energy positions represent less than 10% of its consumption in 2027, within the limits of its energy policy. Block caving: In July 2026, Cerro Lindo began block caving operations in an initial sector of the mine, the first application of this method at Nexa. The operation started in an area of high geotechnical complexity, where the method has been successfully validated in the initial sector, and continues to ramp up gradually over the next 18 months. The operation is supported by real-time geotechnical instrumentation and microseismic monitoring of the production and caving zones. Block caving enables the mining of this geotechnically complex area and requires significantly less drilling, blasting and ground support than conventional sublevel stoping.
