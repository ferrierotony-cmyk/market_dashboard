II. Repurchase of Shares and Tender Offer for Own Shares 1. Purpose of the purchase, etc. The Company, as stated in the “Dividends” section posted on its website, regards the enhancement of shareholders’ interests as one of its important management policies, and remains committed to improving its corporate structure and increasing its corporate value in order to achieve sustainable growth. With regard to dividends, the Company strives to increase its dividend payments on a stable and continuous basis. In order to continue to survive intense competition and to transform itself into a mobility company, the Company will use retained earnings to invest in next-generation growth drivers, including environmental technologies to realize a carbon-neutral society and safety technologies for the safety and security of customers, and to create benefits for all stakeholders, including employees, business partners, and local communities. The Company’s basic policy is to pay dividends of surplus ( jouyokin no haitou ) twice a year as an interim dividend and a year-end dividend, and the Company resolves to pay these dividends at a meeting of the board of directors pursuant to the Articles of Incorporation. In the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026, the Company paid an interim dividend of 45 yen per share and a year-end dividend of 50 yen per share, with a payout ratio (consolidated) of 32.1%. The Company’s Articles of Incorporation provide that the Company may, pursuant to Article 165, Paragraph 2 of the Companies Act, repurchase its shares by a resolution of the board of directors without a resolution of the general meeting of shareholders. The purpose of this provision is to implement a flexible capital policy that is responsive to the business environment. The Company has been continuously repurchasing its shares to date as shown in the table above, and in light of the market’s valuations and current price level of the Company’s common stock and with the aim of further improving capital efficiency, the Company resolved at the meeting of the board of directors held on August 4, 2026 to conduct a repurchase of shares with the total number of shares to be repurchased set at 500,000,000 shares (representing 4.22% of the total number of issued shares (excluding treasury shares) as of the date of the resolution), the total repurchase price set at 1,000 billion yen, and the period of repurchase set to run from August 5, 2026 to August 4, 2027, and is currently conducting such repurchase of shares (Note 4). Meanwhile, the Company has several shareholders who hold the Company’s common stock as policy-held shares (the “ Policy Holding Shareholders ”). In particular, the respective non-life insurance companies that are shareholders of the Company have announced policies to reduce their policy-held shares. Under these circumstances, in early September 2026, the Company began considering repurchasing a portion of the Company’s common stock held by the Policy Holding Shareholders, as treasury shares, because if the Policy Holding Shareholders sell the Company’s common stock in the market, a certain number of the Company’s common stock would be released into the market, causing a temporary deterioration in the supply and demand for the Company’s common stock and a possible negative impact on the stock price, and if the Company repurchases the Company’s common stock as treasury shares, it would contribute to the improvement of the Company’s capital efficiency, including earnings per share (EPS) and return on equity (ROE). With regard to the specific method of the repurchase of shares, the Company took into consideration the transparency of transactions and the status of transactions in the market, and concluded that it is appropriate to conduct the repurchase of shares by way of a tender offer for the following reasons: (i) a tender offer will provide shareholders other than the Prospective Tendering Shareholders (as defined below) with a certain period of time to consider the offer, and will also give them the opportunity to decide, based on market price trends, whether to accept the Company’s repurchase of shares; (ii) a purchase made through statutory tender offer procedures will ensure the transparency of transactions; (iii) a tender offer is an off-market transaction with relatively little impact on the liquidity of the Company’s common stock in the market; and (iv) the purchase of own shares through a market purchase or off-auction trading requires that the purchase price be set at the market price, does not permit a purchase at a certain discount from the market price, and is therefore not a superior option to a tender offer. In respect of the discount rate, in order to grasp the status of the discount rate in a certain number of recent similar projects, among the 59 cases of tender offers for own shares for which settlement was completed between October 2024 and September 2026 (the “ Reference Cases ”), in 52 cases (excluding a total of 7 cases involving share repurchases through tender offers conducted as part of going-private transactions, cases in which a premium was set, and cases in which the tender offer price was determined using a share valuation report) (of which a discount rate of 8% (the figures are rounded to the nearest whole number in calculating the discount rates for the Reference Cases) or more and less than 10% was set in 1 case, a discount rate of 10% was set in 47 cases, and a discount rate of 11% or more was set in 4 cases), the most common discount rate was 10%. By reference to this, the Company considered a discount rate of 10% was typical and at a reasonable level, and determined that it is appropriate to set the discount rate at 10% in the Tender Offer. Furthermore, with respect to the price of the Company’s common stock that serves as the basis for the discount, the Company considered that while adopting a smoothed value, such as the average stock price over a certain period, is considered to eliminate the influence of extraordinary factors including temporary stock price fluctuations, and thus provides an objective and reasonable basis for calculation, in order to reduce the possibility of the market price falling below the Tender Offer Price due to fluctuations during the period for repurchase, etc. of the Tender Offer (the “ Tender Offer Period ”), the Tender Offer Price needs to be at a level that is at a discount even to the latest market stock price. Accordingly, the Company considered it reasonable, and thus determined, to set the price at the lower of: (i) the closing price of the Company’s common stock on the TSE Prime Market on the business day immediately preceding the date of the meeting of the board of directors at which the Tender Offer Price would be finally determined; and (ii) the simple average closing price of the Company’s common stock on the TSE Prime Market for the past one month up to the immediately preceding business day.
