Beyond Meat President and CEO Ethan Brown commented, “Our third quarter results represent measurable progress. Total net revenues came in at the high end of our guidance range, gross margin was our highest in five years, Adjusted EBITDA loss narrowed and we used less cash year-over year.” Brown continued, “O ur international retail channel registered a third consecutive quarter of growth, led by strong performance in Europe. More broadly, we believe we are taking the right steps as we pursue stabilization of U.S. revenues while aggressively driving enterprise-wide cost reduction, including through actions taken in the quarter to substantially reduce certain long-term lease obligations.” Third Quarter 2026 Total net revenues decreased 8.1% to $64.5 million in the third quarter of 2026, compared to $70.2 million in the year-ago period. The decrease in total net revenues was primarily driven by a 10.9% decrease in volume of products sold, partially offset by a 3.2% increase in total net revenue per pound. The decrease in volume of products sold was primarily driven by lower sales of burger products to Quick Service Restaurant (“QSR”) customers in the international foodservice channel, and by reduced points of distribution and weak category demand in the U.S. foodservice and retail channels. The increase in total net revenue per pound was primarily driven by higher price realization on certain of the Company’s products, lower trade discounts and changes in product sales mix, partially offset by unfavorable changes in foreign currency exchange rates. U.S. retail channel net revenues decreased 13.2% to $24.8 million in the third quarter of 2026, compared to $28.5 million in the year-ago period. The decrease in U.S. retail channel net revenues was primarily driven by a 20.8% decrease in volume of products sold, partially offset by a 9.5% increase in net revenue per pound. The decrease in volume of products sold was primarily driven by lower sales of burger and dinner sausage products to club channels, reduced sales of breakfast sausage products, reduced points of distribution within certain sales channels and overall weak category demand. The increase in net revenue per pound was primarily driven by changes in product sales mix, higher price realization on certain of the Company’s products and lower trade discounts. U.S. foodservice channel net revenues decreased 9.7% to $9.5 million in the third quarter of 2026, compared to $10.5 million in the year-ago period. The decrease in U.S. foodservice channel net revenues was primarily driven by a 12.6% decrease in volume of products sold, partially offset by a 3.3% increase in net revenue per pound. The decrease in volume of products sold was primarily driven by reduced points of distribution and weak category demand. The increase in net revenue per pound was primarily driven by lower trade discounts and changes in product sales mix, partially offset by lower price realization on certain of the Company’s products. International retail channel net revenues increased 13.3% to $17.9 million in the third quarter of 2026, compared to $15.8 million in the year-ago period. The increase in international retail channel net revenues was primarily driven by a 17.4% increase in volume of products sold, partially offset by a 3.4% decrease in net revenue per pound. The increase in volume of products sold was primarily driven by increased sales of burger and ground beef products in European markets, and, to a lesser extent, increased sales of burger products in Canada. The decrease in net revenue per pound was primarily driven by higher trade discounts and unfavorable changes in foreign currency exchange rates, partially offset by higher price realization on certain of the Company’s products and changes in product sales mix. International foodservice channel net revenues decreased 19.4% to $12.4 million in the third quarter of 2026, compared to $15.3 million in the year-ago period. The decrease in international foodservice channel net revenues was primarily driven by a 17.5% decrease in volume of products sold and a 2.5% decrease in net revenue per pound. The decrease in volume of products sold was primarily driven by lower sales of burger products to certain QSR customers. The decrease in net revenue per pound was primarily driven by changes in product sales mix and unfavorable changes in foreign currency exchange rates, partially offset by lower trade discounts and higher price realization on certain of the Company’s products. About Beyond Meat Beyond Meat, Inc. (NASDAQ: BYND), otherwise known as Beyond The Plant Protein Company TM , is a plant protein company offering a portfolio of plant-based products made from simple ingredients without GMOs, no added hormones or antibiotics, and 0 mg of cholesterol per serving. Founded in 2009, Beyond Meat’s core products are designed to have the same taste and texture as animal-based meat while being better for people and the planet. Beyond Meat’s brand promise, Eat What You Love®, represents a strong belief that there is a better way to feed our future and that the positive choices we all make, no matter how small, can have a great impact on our personal health and the health of our planet. By shifting from animal-based protein to plant-based protein, we can positively impact four growing global issues: human health, climate change, constraints on natural resources and animal welfare. financial measures set forth below in assessing its operating performance and in its financial communications. Management believes these non-GAAP financial measures provide useful additional information to investors about current trends in the Company's operations and are useful for period-over-period comparisons of operations. In addition, management uses these non-GAAP financial measures to assess operating performance and for business planning purposes. Management also believes these measures are widely used by investors, securities analysts, rating agencies and other parties in evaluating companies in the Company’s industry as a measure of its operational performance. These non-GAAP financial measures should not be considered in isolation or as substitutes for the comparable GAAP measures. In addition, these non-GAAP financial measures may not be computed in the same manner as similarly titled measures used by other companies. “Adjusted loss from operations” is defined as loss from operations adjusted to exclude, when applicable, costs and gains attributable to special items, including non-cash charges related to the cessation of our operational activities in China, costs related to the partial lease termination of a portion of the Campus Headquarters in 2025, non-cash accelerated rent expense in connection with the 2026 partial lease termination, non-cash loss from impairment of long-lived assets, litigation-related accruals and reversals, and settlement related to dispute with former co-manufacturer, which are those items deemed not to be reflective of the Company’s ongoing normal business activities. “Adjusted operating margin” is defined as Adjusted loss from operations divided by total net revenues. “Adjusted net loss” is defined as net loss adjusted to exclude, when applicable, non-cash charges related to the cessation of our operational activities in China, costs related to the partial lease termination of a portion of the Campus Headquarters in 2025, non-cash accelerated rent expense in connection with the 2026 partial lease termination, non-cash loss from impairment of long-lived assets, litigation-related accruals and reversals, remeasurement of delayed draw term loan warrant liability, remeasurement of derivative liability, gain on debt extinguishment, and settlement related to dispute with former co-manufacturer, which are those items deemed not to be reflective of the Company’s ongoing normal business activities. “Adjusted net loss per diluted common share” is defined as Adjusted net loss divided by the weighted average number of diluted common shares outstanding. The Company considers Adjusted loss from operations, Adjusted operating margin, Adjusted net loss and Adjusted net loss per diluted common share to be useful indicators of operating performance because excluding special items allows for period-over-period comparisons of its ongoing operations. Adjusted net loss per diluted common share is a performance measure and should not be used as a measure of liquidity. “Adjusted EBITDA” is defined as net income (loss) adjusted to exclude, when applicable, income tax expense (benefit), interest expense, depreciation and amortization expense, share-based compensation expense, litigation-related accruals and reversals, non-cash charges related to the cessation of our operational activities in China, costs related to the 2025 partial lease termination of a portion of the Campus Headquarters, non-cash accelerated rent expense in connection with the 2026 partial lease termination, non-cash loss from impairment of long-lived assets, remeasurement of delayed draw term loan warrant liability, remeasurement of derivative liability, settlement related to dispute with former co-manufacturer, and Other, net, including interest income, gain on debt extinguishment, foreign currency transaction gains and losses, and the reclassification of cumulative foreign currency translation losses from accumulated other comprehensive loss to Other (expense) income, net upon the cessation of our operational activities in China. “Adjusted EBITDA as a % of total net revenues” is defined as Adjusted EBITDA divided by total net revenues. There are a number of limitations related to the use of Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA as a % of total net revenues rather than their most directly comparable GAAP measures. Some of these limitations are: • Adjusted EBITDA does not reflect income tax payments that reduce cash available to us; • Adjusted EBITDA does not reflect interest expense, or the cash required to service our debt, which reduces cash available to us; • Adjusted EBITDA excludes depreciation and amortization expense and, although these are non-cash expenses, the assets being depreciated may have to be replaced in the future increasing our cash requirements; • Adjusted EBITDA does not reflect share-based compensation expense and therefore does not include all of our compensation costs; • Adjusted EBITDA does not reflect litigation-related accruals or reversals, which may increase or decrease operating expenses and may, depending on the outcome of the underlying litigation, affect cash available to us; • Adjusted EBITDA does not reflect non-cash charges and reclassification of cumulative foreign currency translation losses from accumulated other comprehensive loss to earnings, related to the cessation of our operational activities in China; • Adjusted EBITDA does not reflect certain cash costs related to the 2025 partial lease termination of a portion of the Campus Headquarters, which reduces cash available to us; • Adjusted EBITDA does not reflect non-cash accelerated rent expense in connection with the 2026 partial lease termination of a portion of the Campus Headquarters, and therefore does not include all of our operating expenses; • Adjusted EBITDA does not reflect non-cash loss from impairment of long-lived assets and therefore does not include all of our operating expenses; • Adjusted EBITDA does not reflect the non-cash impact of the remeasurement of delayed draw term loan warrant liability; • Adjusted EBITDA does not reflect the non-cash impact of the remeasurement of derivative liability; • Adjusted EBITDA does not reflect Other, net, including interest income, gain on debt extinguishment, and foreign currency transaction gains and losses, that may increase or decrease cash available to us; • Adjusted EBITDA excludes the settlement of a dispute with a former co-manufacturer which decreased operating expenses; and • other companies, including companies in our industry, may calculate Adjusted EBITDA differently, which reduces its usefulness as a comparative measure.
